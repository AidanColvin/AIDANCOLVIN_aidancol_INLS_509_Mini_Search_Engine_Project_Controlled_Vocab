Depakote, Merrem

Depakote with Merrem: valproate levels drop 60 to 100% within a day: breakthrough seizures

Why D: The Warnings and Precautions section of the Depakote label addresses this combination: valproate levels drop 60 to 100% within a day: breakthrough seizures. Clinical rulebook row 32.

5.15 Interaction with Carbapenem Antibiotics Carbapenem antibiotics (for example, ertapenem, imipenem, meropenem; this is not a complete list) may reduce serum valproate concentrations to subtherapeutic levels, resulting in loss of seizure control.

From the Depakote label, Warnings and Precautions section.
Open on DailyMed

Why it happens

Carbapenems block the enzyme that recycles valproate glucuronide, so valproate levels crash within 24 hours (efficacy loss), and extra valproate does not restore them.

What to do

Avoid; choose a non-carbapenem antibiotic or add another antiseizure drug for the course.

Tylenol with Codeine, Paxil

Tylenol with Codeine with Paxil: less pain relief; more parent-drug side effects

Why C: The Warnings section of the Tylenol with Codeine label addresses this combination: less pain relief; more parent-drug side effects. Clinical rulebook row 8, 35.

Risks of Concomitant Use or Discontinuation of Cytochrome P450 2D6 Inhibitors The concomitant use of acetaminophen and codeine phosphate tablets with all cytochrome P450 2D6 inhibitors (e.g., amiodarone, quinidine) may result in an increase in codeine plasma concentrations and a decrease in active metabolite morphine plasma concentration which could result in an analgesic efficacy reduction or symptoms of opioid withdrawal.

From the Tylenol with Codeine label, Warnings section.
Open on DailyMed

Why it happens

CYP2D6 turns codeine into morphine and tramadol into its active opioid metabolite. Blocking it reduces pain relief, and for tramadol leaves more parent drug with its seizure and serotonin effects.

What to do

Expect weaker analgesia; watch for serotonin effects with tramadol, and consider a different analgesic.

Microzide, Zoloft, Tegretol

3 drugs that lower sodium: risk of low sodium (hyponatremia)

Why D: The Drug Interactions section of the Microzide label addresses this combination: risk of low sodium (hyponatremia). Clinical rulebook row 17.

7.7 Carbamazepine Concomitant use of carbamazepine and hydrochlorothiazide has been associated with the risk of symptomatic hyponatremia.

From the Microzide label, Drug Interactions section.
Open on DailyMed

Why it happens

Thiazides make the kidney lose sodium; SSRIs, SNRIs, carbamazepine, and oxcarbazepine cause the body to hold water (an SIADH-like effect). Together sodium can fall enough to cause confusion, falls, or seizures, most often in older women.

What to do

Check sodium at baseline and within 2 weeks; choose a non-thiazide diuretic if sodium falls.

Adempas, Imdur

Imdur with Adempas: severe, refractory low blood pressure

Why E: The Contraindications section of the Adempas label addresses this combination: severe, refractory low blood pressure. Clinical rulebook row 21.

4 CONTRAINDICATIONS • Pregnancy ( 4.1 ) • Use with nitrates or nitric oxide donors in any form ( 4.2 , 7.1 ) • Use with PDE inhibitors ( 2.6 , 4.3 , 7.1 ) • Patients with concomitant use of other soluble guanylate cyclase (sGC) stimulators.

From the Adempas label, Contraindications section.
Open on DailyMed

Why it happens

Nitrates release nitric oxide, which raises cGMP; PDE5 inhibitors block cGMP breakdown (riociguat stimulates its production). Together blood pressure can fall sharply and not respond to treatment. The labels contraindicate these combinations.

What to do

Do not use together. Allow 24 hours after sildenafil or vardenafil and 48 hours after tadalafil before any nitrate.

Adempas, Imdur

2 drugs: risk of low blood pressure on standing, fainting, and falls

Why C: The Warnings and Precautions section of the Adempas label addresses this combination: risk of low blood pressure on standing, fainting, and falls. Clinical rulebook row 22.

Consider the potential for symptomatic hypotension or ischemia in patients with hypovolemia, severe left ventricular outflow obstruction, resting hypotension, autonomic dysfunction, or concomitant treatment with antihypertensives or strong CYP and P-gp/BCRP inhibitors [see Drug Interactions (7.2) and Clinical Pharmacology (12.3) ] .

From the Adempas label, Warnings and Precautions section.
Open on DailyMed

Why it happens

Each drug lowers blood pressure or blocks the reflex that holds it up when standing. Together they can cause orthostatic hypotension, fainting, and falls.

What to do

Start low, raise one drug at a time, check standing blood pressure, and tell the patient to stand up slowly.
