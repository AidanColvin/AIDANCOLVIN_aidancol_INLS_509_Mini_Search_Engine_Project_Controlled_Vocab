## Overview
- Type: comparative product review of two drug information packages.
- Scope: academic use. Mobile apps not discussed.
- Vendors:
  - Lexicomp Online: Wolters Kluwer Health, Philadelphia, PA; http://www.lexi.com
  - Micromedex 2.0: Truven Health Analytics, Ann Arbor, MI; http://micromedex.com
  - Pricing for both: various options for individuals, academic institutions, and businesses; contact vendor.
- The vendors provided trial access to both full packages (Acknowledgments).

## Key definitions (verbatim)
> A "package" of drug information combines multiple tertiary information items such as reference books, handbooks, and interactive tools; librarians select specific items to license from the options available in a package.

> Pharmacogenomics—variation in drug response based on genetic makeup

## How to evaluate a package (reviewer's advice)
- Get current item lists from publishers. Items are added often, so any list goes out of date.
- Use *Drug Information: A Guide to Current Resources* [1] to understand each item's content.
- Use the *Basic Resources for Pharmacy Education* [2] list, or similar lists for other disciplines, to judge each item's relevance to the library's academic programs.

## Lexicomp

### Content (over 25 items)
- 6 sources of monographs on prescription and over-the-counter drugs
- 2 books on international monographs
- Single books on herbal monographs, patient education (adult and pediatric), pregnancy and lactation, toxicology, drug allergies, lab and diagnostic tests, and pharmacogenomics

### Interactive tools
- Pill identifier
- Oral and topical drug interaction tool
- More than 100 clinical calculators
- 2 intravenous-drug interactions tools

### Duplication
- Lexicomp's editorial team produces some items. Other items are licensed from other publishers, so some content repeats.
- Examples: American Society of Health-System Pharmacists Essentials and Lexidrugs come from separate publishers but give similar brief drug monographs. Two intravenous-drug interactions tools come from two publishers.
- The reviewer calls this duplication useful. Pharmacy information experts recommend that pharmacists check several items from different publishers when answering drug information queries [3, 4].

### Unique aspects
- Lexidrugs gives audio pronunciation of generic drug names.
- Lexidrugs links to freely available guidelines. Example: Infectious Diseases Society of America skin infection guidelines inside an antibiotic monograph.
- Lexicomp's editorial team writes extensive geriatric and neonatal/pediatric monographs, "which are not well-covered elsewhere."
- The Pharmacogenomics book is detailed and frequently updated. It summarizes the research to help novices understand the reasoning behind clinical choices.

### Search and navigation
- Single search box. Filters to all text or to eleven specific categories (uses, adverse reactions, contraindications, warnings, etc.).
- Supports quick answers to questions like "What drugs cause adverse effect X?" and "In the United States, what drugs are approved to treat disease Y?"
- Explore feature: browse books; view indexes, editorial boards, authors, and methods and frequency of updates.
- The reviewer says this item information is "extremely difficult" to reach in Micromedex, and that it helps in judging an item's reliability.
- User Guide, Training Videos, and Quick Start Guide are easy to reach from the main search screen.

## Micromedex 2.0

### Content (more than thirty items)
- Three books of over-the-counter and prescription drug monographs (one focused on pediatric and neonatal patients)
- One herbal monograph source
- Books on pregnancy and lactation, poisoning or toxicology, laboratory tests, evidence-based information on diseases, and new drugs and drugs in development
- Physician's Desk Reference
- RED BOOK (drug pricing)
- Patient care handouts

### Interactive tools
- Twenty clinical calculators
- Oral or topical drug interactions
- Intravenous compatibility
- Pill identification

### Structure: less duplication than Lexicomp
- The editorial team creates some items; others are licensed from outside publishers.
- Instead of duplicate items, Micromedex pairs a summary item (brief information) with a comprehensive item (in-depth information).
- Example: ToxPoints gives guidance for managing an emerging poisoning event. POISONDEX gives long-term management and research evidence on the mechanism of poisoning.

### Unique features
- DRUGDEX gives continually updated evidence on the relative efficacy of two drugs for the same condition. The reviewer notes this literature is hard to find.
- "Drug tools" displays two drug monographs side by side. The reviewer says no other drug information package provides this.
- Contextual (deep) linking between items. Examples: a link to patient care handouts in a monograph's patient education section; a link to an international list of trade names in a monograph's drug names section.

### Search and interface
- Single search screen. Interactive tools run across a top bar.
- Search can be limited to items focused on drugs, diseases, or toxicology.
- Without that limit, results can be confusing, and novices can be overwhelmed by the volume.
- Results split into two halves:
  - Top half: links to the best-matching items, reordered by the query and each item's relevance.
  - Bottom half: results from every item in the subscription, even when only tenuously relevant.
