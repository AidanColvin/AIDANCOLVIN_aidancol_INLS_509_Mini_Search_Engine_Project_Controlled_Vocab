  1. DConsider changing therapy — Dangerous: avoid, or use only with a specific reason and close monitoring.

    Coumadin, Diflucan

    Coumadin with Diflucan: INR rises: risk of serious bleeding

    Why D: Class rule: INR rises: risk of serious bleeding. Clinical rulebook row 10.

    Why it happens

    These drugs block CYP2C9, which clears S-warfarin (the more potent half of warfarin). The INR can double within days; amiodarone's effect lasts for weeks after it stops.

    What to do

    Choose another drug if possible; otherwise lower the warfarin dose (often 25 to 50%) and check the INR within 3 to 5 days.

    FDA labels: Coumadin · Diflucan · Phansalkar et al., JAMIA 2012: high-priority drug-drug interactions (ONC expert panel)

  2. DConsider changing therapy — Dangerous: avoid, or use only with a specific reason and close monitoring.

    Dilantin, Coumadin

    Coumadin with Dilantin: INR swings both ways: bleeding risk first, then clotting risk; phenytoin levels may rise

    Why D: Class rule: INR swings both ways: bleeding risk first, then clotting risk; phenytoin levels may rise. Clinical rulebook row 10, 33.

    Why it happens

    Phenytoin first displaces warfarin from albumin and raises the INR (bleeding risk), then induces its metabolism and lowers the INR (clot risk); warfarin can also raise phenytoin levels.

    What to do

    Check the INR and phenytoin levels often when either drug is started, stopped, or changed.

    FDA labels: Dilantin · Coumadin

    Dilantin, Prozac, Diflucan

    Dilantin with 2 drugs that raise phenytoin levels: phenytoin levels rise: toxicity (unsteadiness, confusion)

    Why D: The Drug Interactions section of the Dilantin label addresses this combination: phenytoin levels rise: toxicity (unsteadiness, confusion).

    Table 3: Drugs Affected by Phenytoin Interacting Agent Examples Drugs whose efficacy is impaired by phenytoin Azoles Fluconazole, ketoconazole, itraconazole, posaconazole, voriconazole Antineoplastic agents Irinotecan, paclitaxel, teniposide Delavirdine Phenytoin can substantially reduce the concentrations of delavirdine.

    From the Dilantin label, Drug Interactions section.Open on DailyMed

    Why it happens

    Phenytoin is cleared by CYP2C9 and CYP2C19 with saturable kinetics, so a small drop in clearance causes a large rise in level. These drugs inhibit those enzymes.

    What to do

    Check a phenytoin level within 1 to 2 weeks and watch for nystagmus, unsteadiness, and confusion.

    FDA labels: Dilantin · Prozac · Diflucan · FDA: Drug Development and Drug Interactions, Table of Substrates, Inhibitors and Inducers

  4. DConsider changing therapy — Dangerous: avoid, or use only with a specific reason and close monitoring.

    Dilantin, Sprintec

    Sprintec with Dilantin: contraceptive failure: unintended pregnancy

    Why D: The Drug Interactions section of the Sprintec label addresses this combination: contraceptive failure: unintended pregnancy. Clinical rulebook row 33.

    Examples Aprepitant, barbiturates, bosentan, carbamazepine, efavirenz, felbamate, griseofulvin, oxcarbazepine, phenytoin, rifampin, rifabutin, rufinamide, topiramate, products containing St.

    From the Sprintec label, Drug Interactions section.Open on DailyMed

    Why it happens

    These drugs induce CYP3A4 and speed the clearance of ethinyl estradiol and progestins, so hormonal contraception can fail (efficacy loss).

    What to do

    Use a non-hormonal or IUD method during the course and for 28 days after the inducer stops.

    FDA labels: Dilantin · Sprintec

  5. CMonitor closely — Clinically significant: adjust the dose or timing, or monitor a named measure.

    Coumadin, Prozac

    2 drugs: risk of serious bleeding, especially stomach and gut bleeding

    Why C: The Warnings and Precautions section of the Coumadin label addresses this combination: risk of serious bleeding, especially stomach and gut bleeding. Clinical rulebook row 9, 12, 13.

    Risk factors for bleeding include high intensity of anticoagulation (INR >4.0), age greater than or equal to 65, history of highly variable INRs, history of gastrointestinal bleeding, hypertension, cerebrovascular disease, anemia, malignancy, trauma, renal impairment, certain genetic factors [see Clinical Pharmacology (12.5) ] , certain concomitant drugs [see Drug Interactions (7) ] , and long duration of warfarin therapy.

    From the Coumadin label, Warnings and Precautions section.Open on DailyMed

    Why it happens

    Anticoagulants slow clotting, aspirin and other antiplatelets stop platelets from sticking, NSAIDs block platelets and injure the stomach lining, and SSRIs and SNRIs drain the serotonin platelets need. Each added drug raises the bleeding risk.

    What to do

    Drop any drug that is not essential (WOEST and AUGUSTUS support dropping aspirin from triple therapy), add a proton pump inhibitor for stomach protection, and watch for black stools, vomiting blood, or anemia.

    FDA labels: Coumadin · Prozac · Dechanont et al., Pharmacoepidemiol Drug Saf 2014: drug interactions in hospital admissions · Dewilde et al., Lancet 2013 (WOEST): dropping aspirin from triple therapy · Lopes et al., NEJM 2019 (AUGUSTUS): antithrombotic therapy after ACS or PCI in atrial fibrillation

  6. BMonitor — Real but manageable: monitor.

    Dilantin, Lipitor

    Lipitor with Dilantin: statin levels fall

    Why B: The Drug Interactions section of the Lipitor label addresses this combination: statin levels fall. Clinical rulebook row 33.

    Rifampin : May reduce atorvastatin plasma concentrations.

    From the Lipitor label, Drug Interactions section.Open on DailyMed

    Why it happens

    CYP3A4 induction lowers statin exposure, so cholesterol lowering weakens (efficacy loss).

    What to do

    Check the lipid panel; use a higher dose or a statin not cleared by CYP3A4.

    FDA labels: Dilantin · Lipitor

  7. AMinor — Minor: usually no action needed.

    Prozac, Diflucan

    QT Prolongation Risk shared by 2 drugs

    Why A: These labels each mention this risk, but no class rule applies to this combination.

    Such conditions include concomitant use of drugs that prolong the QT interval; hypokalemia or hypomagnesemia; recent myocardial infarction, uncompensated heart failure, bradyarrhythmias, and other significant arrhythmias; and conditions that predispose to increased fluoxetine exposure (overdose, hepatic impairment, use of CYP2D6 inhibitors, CYP2D6 poor metabolizer status, or use of other highly protein-bound drugs).

    From the Prozac label, Warnings and Precautions section.Open on DailyMed

    What this means: QT prolongation

    Each of these drugs can delay the heart's electrical recovery after a beat, seen as a longer QT interval on an ECG. Together the delay can add up and trigger torsades de pointes, a dangerous heart rhythm.

    Source: National Heart, Lung, and Blood Institute, NIH
