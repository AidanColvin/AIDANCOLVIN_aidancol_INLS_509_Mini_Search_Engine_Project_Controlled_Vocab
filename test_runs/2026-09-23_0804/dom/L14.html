    Duragesic, Valium, Restoril, Lyrica, Phenergan, Zanaflex

    6 CNS depressants: risk of oversedation, slowed breathing (respiratory depression), falls, and overdose

    Why D: The Boxed Warning section of the Duragesic label addresses this combination: risk of oversedation, slowed breathing (respiratory depression), falls, and overdose. Clinical rulebook row 1, 2.

    Risks From Concomitant Use With Benzodiazepines Or Other CNS Depressants Concomitant use of opioids with benzodiazepines or other central nervous system (CNS) depressants, including alcohol, may result in profound sedation, respiratory depression, coma, and death.

    From the Duragesic label, Boxed Warning section.Open on DailyMed

    Why it happens

    Each drug depresses the brain on its own; together the effects add up. Opioids with benzodiazepines, gabapentinoids, or other sedatives can stop breathing: both opioid and benzodiazepine labels carry a boxed warning on this combination.

    What to do

    Use the lowest doses for the shortest time, avoid adding more sedatives, and watch for sleepiness and slow or shallow breathing. With an opioid, co-prescribe naloxone.

    • Duragesic with 2 benzodiazepines: boxed warning: profound sedation, respiratory depression, coma, death. Opioids and benzodiazepines both suppress the brainstem's drive to breathe. Both drug classes carry an FDA boxed warning against this combination.
    • Duragesic with Lyrica: risk of respiratory depression. Gabapentin and pregabalin add to opioid respiratory depression; the FDA added this warning to both in 2019. Both are cleared by the kidney, so kidney injury raises exposure.

    FDA labels: Duragesic · Valium · Restoril · Lyrica · Phenergan · Zanaflex · Phansalkar et al., JAMIA 2012: high-priority drug-drug interactions (ONC expert panel)

  3. DConsider changing therapy — Dangerous: avoid, or use only with a specific reason and close monitoring.

    Valium, Restoril

    Duplicate: Valium (diazepam) and Restoril (temazepam): one is the active metabolite of the other: combined diazepam 30 mg/day and temazepam 30 mg/day

    Why D: Hidden duplication: the entries name one drug differently, so the doses add up unnoticed.

    Metabolism Diazepam is N-demethylated by CYP3A4 and 2C19 to the active metabolite N-desmethyldiazepam, and is hydroxylated by CYP3A4 to the active metabolite temazepam.

    From the Valium label, clinical_pharmacology section.Open on DailyMed

    Why it happens

    Both entries deliver the same active drug, so the patient gets the combined dose. One is the active metabolite of the other.

    What to do

    Confirm whether both lines are meant. If one is a duplicate entry, remove it; if both are real, treat the combined total as the dose.

    FDA labels: Valium · Restoril
