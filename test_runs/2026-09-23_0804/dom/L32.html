    Trexall, Bactrim DS

    Trexall with Bactrim DS: bone-marrow suppression, even at low weekly doses

    Why D: Class rule: bone-marrow suppression, even at low weekly doses. Clinical rulebook row 30.

    Why it happens

    Trimethoprim blocks the same folate enzyme (DHFR) as methotrexate, and sulfamethoxazole reduces methotrexate's kidney clearance. Fatal pancytopenia is reported even with low weekly methotrexate.

    What to do

    Avoid; choose another antibiotic. If unavoidable, check blood counts and consider leucovorin.

    FDA labels: Trexall · Bactrim DS · Phansalkar et al., JAMIA 2012: high-priority drug-drug interactions (ONC expert panel)

  2. DConsider changing therapy — Dangerous: avoid, or use only with a specific reason and close monitoring.

    Imuran, Zyloprim

    Imuran with Zyloprim: thiopurine levels rise: severe bone marrow suppression (myelosuppression)

    Why D: The Precautions section of the Imuran label addresses this combination: thiopurine levels rise: severe bone marrow suppression (myelosuppression). Clinical rulebook row 29.

    Careful dosage instructions should be given to the patient, especially when azathioprine is being administered in the presence of impaired renal function or concomitantly with allopurinol (see Drug Interactions subsection and DOSAGE AND ADMINISTRATION ).

    From the Imuran label, Precautions section.Open on DailyMed

    Why it happens

    Allopurinol blocks xanthine oxidase, a main route for clearing 6-mercaptopurine (azathioprine's active form), shunting it to toxic thioguanine nucleotides.

    What to do

    Reduce azathioprine or mercaptopurine to one-third to one-quarter of the dose, or avoid; check blood counts often.

    FDA labels: Imuran · Zyloprim · Phansalkar et al., JAMIA 2012: high-priority drug-drug interactions (ONC expert panel)

  3. CMonitor closely — Clinically significant: adjust the dose or timing, or monitor a named measure.

    Trexall, Motrin

    2 drugs: risk of acute kidney injury

    Why C: The Warnings section of the Motrin label addresses this combination: risk of acute kidney injury. Clinical rulebook row 14.

    Patients at greatest risk of this reaction are those with impaired renal function, heart failure, liver dysfunction, those taking diuretics and ACE inhibitors, and the elderly.

    From the Motrin label, Warnings section.Open on DailyMed

    Why it happens

    NSAIDs remove the prostaglandins that keep blood flowing into the kidney, ACE inhibitors and ARBs lower the pressure that keeps it filtering, and diuretics reduce blood volume (the "triple whammy"). Aminoglycosides, vancomycin, and other listed drugs injure the kidney directly.

    What to do

    Avoid the NSAID if possible, keep the patient hydrated, and check creatinine within a week, sooner in the hospital.

    FDA labels: Trexall · Motrin · Lapi et al., BMJ 2013: diuretic + ACE inhibitor/ARB + NSAID and acute kidney injury

  4. CMonitor closely — Clinically significant: adjust the dose or timing, or monitor a named measure.

    Trexall, Motrin

    Trexall with Motrin: methotrexate clearance falls: kidney and marrow toxicity

    Why C: Class rule: methotrexate clearance falls: kidney and marrow toxicity. Clinical rulebook row 30.

    Why it happens

    NSAIDs reduce the kidney's secretion of methotrexate (OAT1 and OAT3) and can lower kidney function, raising methotrexate levels. The risk is highest with high methotrexate doses.

    What to do

    Monitor blood counts, kidney function, and liver tests; avoid NSAIDs around high-dose methotrexate.

    FDA labels: Trexall · Motrin
