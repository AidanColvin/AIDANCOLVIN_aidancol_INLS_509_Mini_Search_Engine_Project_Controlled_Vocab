  1. DConsider changing therapy — Dangerous: avoid, or use only with a specific reason and close monitoring.

    Glucotrol, Lantus, Inderal, Levaquin, Ozempic

    5 drugs: risk of low blood sugar (hypoglycemia)

    Why D: The Precautions section of the Glucotrol label addresses this combination: risk of low blood sugar (hypoglycemia). Clinical rulebook row 26.

    Increase frequency of glucose monitoring with changes to: insulin dosage, co-administered glucose lowering medications, meal pattern, physical activity; and in patients with renal or hepatic impairment and hypoglycemia unawareness.

    From the Lantus label, Warnings and Precautions section.Open on DailyMed

    Why it happens

    Insulin and sulfonylureas lower glucose regardless of the level; adding more glucose-lowering drugs stacks the effect. Fluoroquinolones carry a label warning for severe blood-sugar swings, and beta-blockers hide the shaking and fast heartbeat that warn of a low.

    What to do

    Lower the insulin or sulfonylurea dose when adding another agent, check glucose more often, and teach the patient to recognize lows without the usual warning signs.

    FDA labels: Glucotrol · Lantus · Inderal · Levaquin · Ozempic · Budnitz et al., NEJM 2011: emergency hospitalizations for adverse drug events in older adults

  2. DConsider changing therapy — Dangerous: avoid, or use only with a specific reason and close monitoring.

    Glucotrol, Bactrim DS

    Glucotrol with Bactrim DS: sulfonylurea levels rise: severe, prolonged low blood sugar

    Why D: The Precautions section of the Glucotrol label addresses this combination: sulfonylurea levels rise: severe, prolonged low blood sugar. Clinical rulebook row 26.

    A potential interaction between oral miconazole and oral hypoglycemic agents leading to severe hypoglycemia has been reported.

    From the Glucotrol label, Precautions section.Open on DailyMed

    Why it happens

    These drugs block CYP2C9 (or CYP3A4), which clears sulfonylureas, so the sulfonylurea accumulates. Severe hypoglycemia lasting more than a day is reported, especially in older adults.

    What to do

    Choose another antibiotic if possible, or lower the sulfonylurea dose and check glucose often during the course.

    FDA labels: Glucotrol · Bactrim DS · Budnitz et al., NEJM 2011: emergency hospitalizations for adverse drug events in older adults

    Ozempic with Glucotrol: slower stomach emptying changes oral drug absorption

    Why A: The Warnings and Precautions section of the Ozempic label addresses this combination: slower stomach emptying changes oral drug absorption.

    5.5 Hypoglycemia with Concomitant Use of Insulin Secretagogues or Insulin Patients receiving OZEMPIC in combination with an insulin secretagogue (e.g., sulfonylurea) or insulin may have an increased risk of hypoglycemia, including severe hypoglycemia [see Adverse Reactions ( 6.1 ), Drug Interactions ( 7 )].

    From the Ozempic label, Warnings and Precautions section.Open on DailyMed

    Why it happens

    GLP-1 agonists slow stomach emptying and can change the timing and amount of oral drug absorption.

    What to do

    Usually minor; monitor glucose, INR, or TSH after starting.

    FDA labels: Glucotrol · Ozempic
