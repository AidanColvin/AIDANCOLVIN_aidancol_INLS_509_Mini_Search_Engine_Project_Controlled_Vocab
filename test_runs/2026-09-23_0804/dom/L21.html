    Nizoral, Halcion

    Halcion with Nizoral: sedative levels rise many-fold: prolonged, deep sedation

    Why E: The Warnings and Precautions section of the Halcion label addresses this combination: sedative levels rise many-fold: prolonged, deep sedation.

    Strong CYP 3A Inhibitors Triazolam is contraindicated in patients receiving strong inhibitors of CYP 3A such as ketoconazole, itraconazole, nefazodone, ritonavir, indinavir, nelfinavir, saquinavir, and lopinavir [see Contraindications (4) , Drug Interactions (7.1) ] .

    From the Halcion label, Warnings and Precautions section.Open on DailyMed

    Why it happens

    Triazolam and oral midazolam depend on CYP3A4; ketoconazole raises triazolam exposure more than 20-fold. The labels contraindicate strong CYP3A4 inhibitors.

    What to do

    Do not use together; choose a benzodiazepine not cleared by CYP3A4 (lorazepam, temazepam) if one is needed.

    FDA labels: Nizoral · Halcion · FDA: Drug Development and Drug Interactions, Table of Substrates, Inhibitors and Inducers

  3. DConsider changing therapy — Dangerous: avoid, or use only with a specific reason and close monitoring.

    Nizoral, Lipitor

    Lipitor with Nizoral: atorvastatin levels rise: risk of muscle injury

    Why D: The Warnings and Precautions section of the Lipitor label addresses this combination: atorvastatin levels rise: risk of muscle injury. Clinical rulebook row 27.

    Concomitant use of cyclosporine, gemfibrozil, tipranavir plus ritonavir, or glecaprevir plus pibrentasvir with atorvastatin calcium tablets is not recommended.

    From the Lipitor label, Warnings and Precautions section.Open on DailyMed

    Why it happens

    Strong CYP3A4 inhibitors raise atorvastatin exposure several-fold. The atorvastatin label limits the dose (20 mg with clarithromycin, itraconazole, or boosted protease inhibitors) and advises avoiding cyclosporine, gemfibrozil, and tipranavir.

    What to do

    Use the lowest atorvastatin dose (at most 20 mg with these), or switch to pravastatin or rosuvastatin; watch for muscle pain and dark urine.

    FDA labels: Nizoral · Lipitor · FDA: Drug Development and Drug Interactions, Table of Substrates, Inhibitors and Inducers

  4. DConsider changing therapy — Dangerous: avoid, or use only with a specific reason and close monitoring.

    Nizoral, Eliquis

    Eliquis with Nizoral: anticoagulant levels rise: risk of major bleeding

    Why D: The Drug Interactions section of the Eliquis label addresses this combination: anticoagulant levels rise: risk of major bleeding. Clinical rulebook row 11.

    (7.2 , 12.3) 7.1 Combined P-gp and Strong CYP3A4 Inhibitors For patients receiving ELIQUIS 5 mg or 10 mg twice daily, the dose of ELIQUIS should be decreased by 50% when coadministered with drugs that are combined P-gp and strong CYP3A4 inhibitors (e.g., ketoconazole, itraconazole, ritonavir) [see Dosage and Administration (2.6) and Clinical Pharmacology (12.3) ] .

    From the Eliquis label, Drug Interactions section.Open on DailyMed

    Why it happens

    These drugs block both CYP3A4 and P-glycoprotein, the two routes that clear apixaban and rivaroxaban, roughly doubling their levels.

    What to do

    Avoid, or use the reduced dose the label specifies (apixaban 2.5 mg twice daily), and watch closely for bleeding.

    FDA labels: Nizoral · Eliquis · Phansalkar et al., JAMIA 2012: high-priority drug-drug interactions (ONC expert panel)

  6. CMonitor closely — Clinically significant: adjust the dose or timing, or monitor a named measure.

    Halcion, Xanax

    2 CNS depressants: risk of oversedation, slowed breathing (respiratory depression), falls, and overdose

    Why C: Class rule: risk of oversedation, slowed breathing (respiratory depression), falls, and overdose. Clinical rulebook row 1, 2.

    WARNING: RISKS FROM CONCOMITANT USE WITH OPIOIDS; ABUSE, MISUSE, AND ADDICTION; and DEPENDENCE AND WITHDRAWAL REACTIONS • Concomitant use of benzodiazepines and opioids may result in profound sedation, respiratory depression, coma, and death.

    From the Halcion label, Boxed Warning section.Open on DailyMed

    Why it happens

    Each drug depresses the brain on its own; together the effects add up. Opioids with benzodiazepines, gabapentinoids, or other sedatives can stop breathing: both opioid and benzodiazepine labels carry a boxed warning on this combination.

    What to do

    Use the lowest doses for the shortest time, avoid adding more sedatives, and watch for sleepiness and slow or shallow breathing. With an opioid, co-prescribe naloxone.

    FDA labels: Halcion · Xanax · Phansalkar et al., JAMIA 2012: high-priority drug-drug interactions (ONC expert panel)
