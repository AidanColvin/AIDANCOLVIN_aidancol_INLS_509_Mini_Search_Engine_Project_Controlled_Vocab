Dilantin, Coumadin

Coumadin with Dilantin: INR swings both ways: bleeding risk first, then clotting risk; phenytoin levels may rise

Why D: Class rule: INR swings both ways: bleeding risk first, then clotting risk; phenytoin levels may rise. Clinical rulebook row 10, 33.

Why it happens

Phenytoin first displaces warfarin from albumin and raises the INR (bleeding risk), then induces its metabolism and lowers the INR (clot risk); warfarin can also raise phenytoin levels.

What to do

Check the INR and phenytoin levels often when either drug is started, stopped, or changed.

Why C: The Warnings and Precautions section of the Coumadin label addresses this combination: risk of serious bleeding, especially stomach and gut bleeding. Clinical rulebook row 9, 12, 13.

Risk factors for bleeding include high intensity of anticoagulation (INR >4.0), age greater than or equal to 65, history of highly variable INRs, history of gastrointestinal bleeding, hypertension, cerebrovascular disease, anemia, malignancy, trauma, renal impairment, certain genetic factors [see Clinical Pharmacology (12.5) ] , certain concomitant drugs [see Drug Interactions (7) ] , and long duration of warfarin therapy.

From the Coumadin label, Warnings and Precautions section.
Open on DailyMed

Why it happens

Anticoagulants slow clotting, aspirin and other antiplatelets stop platelets from sticking, NSAIDs block platelets and injure the stomach lining, and SSRIs and SNRIs drain the serotonin platelets need. Each added drug raises the bleeding risk.

What to do

Drop any drug that is not essential (WOEST and AUGUSTUS support dropping aspirin from triple therapy), add a proton pump inhibitor for stomach protection, and watch for black stools, vomiting blood, or anemia.
