  1. DConsider changing therapy — Dangerous: avoid, or use only with a specific reason and close monitoring.

    Prograf, Diflucan, Zofran

    3 QT-prolonging drugs: risk of QT prolongation and torsades de pointes

    Why D: The Warnings and Precautions section of the Prograf label addresses this combination: risk of QT prolongation and torsades de pointes. Clinical rulebook row 23.

    Coadministration of other drugs known to prolong the QT interval and which are metabolized via the enzyme CYP3A4 such as erythromycin, pimozide, and quinidine are contraindicated in patients receiving fluconazole.

    From the Diflucan label, Contraindications section.Open on DailyMed

    Why it happens

    Each drug slows the heart's electrical recovery (the QT interval). Together the effect adds up and can trigger torsades de pointes, a dangerous heart rhythm.

    What to do

    Get a baseline ECG and keep potassium and magnesium normal. Stop or switch a drug if the QTc passes 500 ms.

    FDA labels: Prograf · Diflucan · Zofran · Dechanont et al., Pharmacoepidemiol Drug Saf 2014: drug interactions in hospital admissions

  2. DConsider changing therapy — Dangerous: avoid, or use only with a specific reason and close monitoring.

    Prograf, Diflucan

    Prograf with Diflucan: immunosuppressant levels rise: kidney and nerve toxicity

    Why D: The Warnings and Precautions section of the Prograf label addresses this combination: immunosuppressant levels rise: kidney and nerve toxicity. Clinical rulebook row 34.

    The risk for nephrotoxicity may increase when tacrolimus is concomitantly administered with CYP3A inhibitors (by increasing tacrolimus whole blood concentrations) or drugs associated with nephrotoxicity (e.g., aminoglycosides, ganciclovir, amphotericin B, cisplatin, nucleotide reverse transcriptase inhibitors, protease inhibitors).

    From the Prograf label, Warnings and Precautions section.Open on DailyMed

    Why it happens

    Tacrolimus and cyclosporine are narrow-margin drugs cleared by CYP3A4 and P-glycoprotein. Azoles, macrolides, diltiazem, and verapamil raise their levels, causing kidney injury, tremor and seizures, high potassium, and high blood pressure.

    What to do

    Reduce the immunosuppressant dose pre-emptively and check trough levels within a few days of starting or stopping the inhibitor.

    FDA labels: Prograf · Diflucan · FDA: Drug Development and Drug Interactions, Table of Substrates, Inhibitors and Inducers · Phansalkar et al., JAMIA 2012: high-priority drug-drug interactions (ONC expert panel)

  3. CMonitor closely — Clinically significant: adjust the dose or timing, or monitor a named measure.

    Diflucan, Zocor

    Zocor with Diflucan: raises the statin level: risk of muscle injury

    Why C: The Warnings and Precautions section of the Zocor label addresses this combination: raises the statin level: risk of muscle injury. Clinical rulebook row 27.

    Simvastatin dosage modifications are recommended for patients taking lomitapide, verapamil, diltiazem, dronedarone, amiodarone, amlodipine or ranolazine [see DOSAGE AND ADMINISTRATION ( 2.5 )].

    From the Zocor label, Warnings and Precautions section.Open on DailyMed

    Why it happens

    These drugs slow simvastatin's clearance. The simvastatin label caps the dose at 10 mg a day with verapamil, diltiazem, or dronedarone, and 20 mg with amiodarone, amlodipine, or ranolazine.

    What to do

    Keep simvastatin at or below the label's cap, or switch to a statin not cleared by CYP3A4 (pravastatin, rosuvastatin).

    FDA labels: Diflucan · Zocor · FDA: Drug Development and Drug Interactions, Table of Substrates, Inhibitors and Inducers

  4. CMonitor closely — Clinically significant: adjust the dose or timing, or monitor a named measure.

    CellCept, Prilosec

    CellCept with Prilosec: less mycophenolate is absorbed (exposure falls 25 to 35%)

    Why C: The Drug Interactions section of the CellCept label addresses this combination: less mycophenolate is absorbed (exposure falls 25 to 35%).

    7 DRUG INTERACTIONS See FPI for drugs that may interfere with systemic exposure and reduce CELLCEPT efficacy: antacids with magnesium or aluminum hydroxide, proton pump inhibitors, drugs that interfere with enterohepatic recirculation, telmisartan, calcium-free phosphate binders.

    From the CellCept label, Drug Interactions section.Open on DailyMed

    Why it happens

    Mycophenolate mofetil needs stomach acid to dissolve; with acid suppression less is absorbed, and exposure to the active drug (mycophenolic acid) falls 25 to 35%.

    What to do

    Monitor for rejection or consider enteric-coated mycophenolate sodium.

    FDA labels: CellCept · Prilosec

    Prograf, Prilosec

    Prograf with Prilosec: tacrolimus levels may rise slightly

    Why A: The Drug Interactions section of the Prograf label addresses this combination: tacrolimus levels may rise slightly.

    Mild or Moderate CYP3A Inhibitors: Clotrimazole, antibiotics (e.g., erythromycin, fluconazole), calcium channel blockers (e.g., verapamil, diltiazem, nifedipine, nicardipine), amiodarone, danazol, ethinyl estradiol, cimetidine, lansoprazole and omeprazole May increase tacrolimus whole blood trough concentrations and increase the risk of serious adverse reactions (e.g., neurotoxicity, QT prolongation) [see Warnings and Precautions (5.6 , 5.11 , 5.12 )].

    From the Prograf label, Drug Interactions section.Open on DailyMed

    Why it happens

    Omeprazole and lansoprazole weakly inhibit CYP2C19 and CYP3A4 and can modestly raise tacrolimus levels, mainly in CYP2C19 poor metabolizers.

    What to do

    Usually no change; check a tacrolimus level if one is due.

    FDA labels: Prograf · Prilosec
