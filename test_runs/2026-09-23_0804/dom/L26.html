  1. DConsider changing therapy — Dangerous: avoid, or use only with a specific reason and close monitoring.

    Depakote, Lamictal, Topamax, Tegretol, Klonopin

    5 CNS depressants: risk of oversedation, slowed breathing (respiratory depression), falls, and overdose

    Why D: Class rule: risk of oversedation, slowed breathing (respiratory depression), falls, and overdose. Clinical rulebook row 1, 2.

    WARNING: RISKS FROM CONCOMITANT USE WITH OPIOIDS; ABUSE, MISUSE, AND ADDICTION; and DEPENDENCE AND WITHDRAWAL REACTIONS Concomitant use of benzodiazepines and opioids may result in profound sedation, respiratory depression, coma, and death.

    From the Klonopin label, Boxed Warning section.Open on DailyMed

    Why it happens

    Each drug depresses the brain on its own; together the effects add up. Opioids with benzodiazepines, gabapentinoids, or other sedatives can stop breathing: both opioid and benzodiazepine labels carry a boxed warning on this combination.

    What to do

    Use the lowest doses for the shortest time, avoid adding more sedatives, and watch for sleepiness and slow or shallow breathing. With an opioid, co-prescribe naloxone.

    FDA labels: Depakote · Lamictal · Topamax · Tegretol · Klonopin · Phansalkar et al., JAMIA 2012: high-priority drug-drug interactions (ONC expert panel)

  2. DConsider changing therapy — Dangerous: avoid, or use only with a specific reason and close monitoring.

    Depakote, Lamictal

    Lamictal with Depakote: lamotrigine levels double: risk of serious rash (Stevens-Johnson)

    Why D: The Boxed Warning section of the Lamictal label addresses this combination: lamotrigine levels double: risk of serious rash (Stevens-Johnson).

    In addition to age, factors that may increase the risk of occurrence or the severity of rash caused by lamotrigine include (1) coadministration of lamotrigine with valproate (includes valproic acid and divalproex sodium), (2) exceeding the recommended initial dose of lamotrigine, or (3) exceeding the recommended dose escalation for lamotrigine, or (4) the presence of the HLA-B*1502 allele.

    From the Lamictal label, Boxed Warning section.Open on DailyMed

    Why it happens

    Valproate inhibits lamotrigine's glucuronidation and roughly doubles its half-life, raising the risk of serious rash including Stevens-Johnson syndrome.

    What to do

    Use the lamotrigine label's valproate dosing (about half the usual dose, slower titration) and teach the patient to report any rash.

    FDA labels: Depakote · Lamictal

    Depakote, Topamax

    Depakote with Topamax: high ammonia with confusion (hyperammonemic encephalopathy) and low body temperature

    Why D: The Warnings and Precautions section of the Depakote label addresses this combination: high ammonia with confusion (hyperammonemic encephalopathy) and low body temperature.

    This adverse reaction can also occur in patients using concomitant topiramate ( 5.11 ) Drug Reaction with Eosinophilia and Systemic Symptoms (DRESS)/Multiorgan hypersensitivity reactions, serious dermatologic reactions, and angioedema: Discontinue divalproex sodium extended-release tablets unless an alternate etiology is established ( 5.12 , 5.13 , 5.14 ) 5.1 Hepatotoxicity General Information on Hepatotoxicity Hepatic failure resulting in fatalities has occurred in patients receiving valproate.

    From the Depakote label, Warnings and Precautions section.Open on DailyMed

    Why it happens

    Both labels warn that the combination can raise ammonia and cause encephalopathy and hypothermia, even when valproate levels are normal.

    What to do

    Check ammonia if the patient becomes lethargic, confused, or vomits; stop one drug if it is high.

    FDA labels: Depakote · Topamax

    Lamictal, Tegretol

    Lamictal with Tegretol: lamotrigine levels fall about 40%

    Why C: The Drug Interactions section of the Lamictal label addresses this combination: lamotrigine levels fall about 40%. Clinical rulebook row 33.

    Carbamazepine and Carbamazepine epoxide ↓ lamotrigine Addition of carbamazepine decreases lamotrigine concentration approximately 40%. ?

    From the Lamictal label, Drug Interactions section.Open on DailyMed

    Why it happens

    These drugs induce lamotrigine's glucuronidation and cut its levels by about 40 to 50%.

    What to do

    Use the label's higher lamotrigine dosing with inducers, and re-adjust if the inducer stops.

    FDA labels: Lamictal · Tegretol

  5. CMonitor closely — Clinically significant: adjust the dose or timing, or monitor a named measure.

    Depakote, Bayer

    Depakote with Bayer: free valproate rises and bleeding risk adds up

    Why C: The Drug Interactions section of the Depakote label addresses this combination: free valproate rises and bleeding risk adds up.

    Drugs for which a potentially important interaction has been observed Aspirin A study involving the co-administration of aspirin at antipyretic doses (11 to 16 mg/kg) with valproate to pediatric patients (n=6) revealed a decrease in protein binding and an inhibition of metabolism of valproate.

    From the Depakote label, Drug Interactions section.Open on DailyMed

    Why it happens

    Aspirin displaces valproate from albumin and slows its metabolism, raising free valproate; valproate can lower platelets, and aspirin blocks them.

    What to do

    Watch for bruising and valproate side effects; check platelets.

    FDA labels: Depakote · Bayer

  6. CMonitor closely — Clinically significant: adjust the dose or timing, or monitor a named measure.

    Depakote, Tegretol

    Depakote with Tegretol: valproate levels fall; carbamazepine epoxide rises

    Why C: The Warnings and Precautions section of the Depakote label addresses this combination: valproate levels fall; carbamazepine epoxide rises. Clinical rulebook row 33.

    The largest of these studies 1 is a prospective cohort study conducted in the United States and United Kingdom that found that children with prenatal exposure to valproate (n=62) had lower IQ scores at age 6 (97 [95% CI 94 to 101]) than children with prenatal exposure to the other antiepileptic drug monotherapy treatments evaluated: lamotrigine (108 [95% CI 105 to 110]), carbamazepine (105 [95% CI 102 to 108]), and phenytoin (108 [95% CI 104 to 112]).

    From the Depakote label, Warnings and Precautions section.Open on DailyMed

    Why it happens

    These drugs induce valproate metabolism and lower its levels, and valproate raises carbamazepine's toxic 10,11-epoxide metabolite.

    What to do

    Check drug levels when either is started or changed, and watch for dizziness, double vision, and unsteadiness.

    FDA labels: Depakote · Tegretol
