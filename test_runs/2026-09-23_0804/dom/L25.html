    Dolophine, Seroquel

    2 CNS depressants: risk of oversedation, slowed breathing (respiratory depression), falls, and overdose

    Why D: The Boxed Warning section of the Dolophine label addresses this combination: risk of oversedation, slowed breathing (respiratory depression), falls, and overdose. Clinical rulebook row 1, 2.

    Risks From Concomitant Use With Benzodiazepines Or Other CNS Depressants Concomitant use of opioids with benzodiazepines or other central nervous system (CNS) depressants, including alcohol, may result in profound sedation, respiratory depression, coma, and death.

    From the Dolophine label, Boxed Warning section.Open on DailyMed

    Why it happens

    Each drug depresses the brain on its own; together the effects add up. Opioids with benzodiazepines, gabapentinoids, or other sedatives can stop breathing: both opioid and benzodiazepine labels carry a boxed warning on this combination.

    What to do

    Use the lowest doses for the shortest time, avoid adding more sedatives, and watch for sleepiness and slow or shallow breathing. With an opioid, co-prescribe naloxone.

    FDA labels: Dolophine · Seroquel · Phansalkar et al., JAMIA 2012: high-priority drug-drug interactions (ONC expert panel)

    Why it happens

    Each drug raises serotonin activity. Together they can cause serotonin syndrome: agitation, tremor, clonus and overactive reflexes, sweating, fever, fast heart rate.

    What to do

    Avoid stacking serotonergic drugs where possible; if needed, use low doses and watch for clonus, tremor, fever, and agitation, especially in the first days after a start or dose increase.

    FDA labels: Dolophine · Zofran · Effexor XR · Dunkley et al., QJM 2003: the Hunter serotonin toxicity criteria

    Dolophine, Seroquel, Zofran, Levaquin, Diflucan

    5 QT-prolonging drugs: risk of QT prolongation and torsades de pointes

    Why D: The Boxed Warning section of the Dolophine label addresses this combination: risk of QT prolongation and torsades de pointes. Clinical rulebook row 23.

    Coadministration of other drugs known to prolong the QT interval and which are metabolized via the enzyme CYP3A4 such as erythromycin, pimozide, and quinidine are contraindicated in patients receiving fluconazole.

    From the Diflucan label, Contraindications section.Open on DailyMed

    Why it happens

    Each drug slows the heart's electrical recovery (the QT interval). Together the effect adds up and can trigger torsades de pointes, a dangerous heart rhythm.

    What to do

    Get a baseline ECG and keep potassium and magnesium normal. Stop or switch a drug if the QTc passes 500 ms.

    FDA labels: Dolophine · Seroquel · Zofran · Levaquin · Diflucan · Dechanont et al., Pharmacoepidemiol Drug Saf 2014: drug interactions in hospital admissions

    Dolophine with Diflucan: opioid levels rise: risk of fatal respiratory depression

    Why D: The Warnings and Precautions section of the Dolophine label addresses this combination: opioid levels rise: risk of fatal respiratory depression. Clinical rulebook row 3.

    5.19 Laboratory Test Interactions False positive urine drug screens for methadone have been reported for several drugs including diphenhydramine, doxylamine, clomipramine, chlorpromazine, thioridazine, quetiapine, and verapamil.

    From the Dolophine label, Warnings and Precautions section.Open on DailyMed

    Why it happens

    These opioids are cleared by CYP3A4. A CYP3A4 inhibitor raises opioid levels (fentanyl and oxycodone roughly 2 to 3 times), and the effect can build over days. The opioid labels carry a boxed warning on CYP3A4 inhibitors.

    What to do

    Avoid if possible; otherwise lower the opioid dose, monitor closely for sedation and slow breathing, and re-adjust when the inhibitor stops.

    FDA labels: Dolophine · Diflucan · FDA: Drug Development and Drug Interactions, Table of Substrates, Inhibitors and Inducers
