Why it happens

Insulin and sulfonylureas lower glucose regardless of the level; adding more glucose-lowering drugs stacks the effect. Fluoroquinolones carry a label warning for severe blood-sugar swings, and beta-blockers hide the shaking and fast heartbeat that warn of a low.

What to do

Lower the insulin or sulfonylurea dose when adding another agent, check glucose more often, and teach the patient to recognize lows without the usual warning signs.

Why D: The Precautions section of the Glucotrol label addresses this combination: sulfonylurea levels rise: severe, prolonged low blood sugar. Clinical rulebook row 26.

A potential interaction between oral miconazole and oral hypoglycemic agents leading to severe hypoglycemia has been reported.

From the Glucotrol label, Precautions section.
Open on DailyMed

Why it happens

These drugs block CYP2C9 (or CYP3A4), which clears sulfonylureas, so the sulfonylurea accumulates. Severe hypoglycemia lasting more than a day is reported, especially in older adults.

What to do

Choose another antibiotic if possible, or lower the sulfonylurea dose and check glucose often during the course.
