  1. DConsider changing therapy — Dangerous: avoid, or use only with a specific reason and close monitoring.

    Norco, Percocet, Ambien, Lunesta

    4 CNS depressants: risk of oversedation, slowed breathing (respiratory depression), falls, and overdose

    Why D: The Boxed Warning section of the Norco label addresses this combination: risk of oversedation, slowed breathing (respiratory depression), falls, and overdose. Clinical rulebook row 1, 2.

    Risks from Concomitant Use with Benzodiazepines or Other CNS Depressants Concomitant use of opioids with benzodiazepines or other central nervous system (CNS) depressants, including alcohol, may result in profound sedation, respiratory depression, coma, and death.

    From the Norco label, Boxed Warning section.Open on DailyMed

    Why it happens

    Each drug depresses the brain on its own; together the effects add up. Opioids with benzodiazepines, gabapentinoids, or other sedatives can stop breathing: both opioid and benzodiazepine labels carry a boxed warning on this combination.

    What to do

    Use the lowest doses for the shortest time, avoid adding more sedatives, and watch for sleepiness and slow or shallow breathing. With an opioid, co-prescribe naloxone.

    FDA labels: Norco · Percocet · Ambien · Lunesta · Phansalkar et al., JAMIA 2012: high-priority drug-drug interactions (ONC expert panel)

  6. CMonitor closely — Clinically significant: adjust the dose or timing, or monitor a named measure.

    Mobic, Aleve

    2 drugs: risk of serious bleeding, especially stomach and gut bleeding

    Why C: Class rule: risk of serious bleeding, especially stomach and gut bleeding. Clinical rulebook row 9, 12, 13.

    Why it happens

    Anticoagulants slow clotting, aspirin and other antiplatelets stop platelets from sticking, NSAIDs block platelets and injure the stomach lining, and SSRIs and SNRIs drain the serotonin platelets need. Each added drug raises the bleeding risk.

    What to do

    Drop any drug that is not essential (WOEST and AUGUSTUS support dropping aspirin from triple therapy), add a proton pump inhibitor for stomach protection, and watch for black stools, vomiting blood, or anemia.

    FDA labels: Mobic · Aleve · Dechanont et al., Pharmacoepidemiol Drug Saf 2014: drug interactions in hospital admissions · Dewilde et al., Lancet 2013 (WOEST): dropping aspirin from triple therapy · Lopes et al., NEJM 2019 (AUGUSTUS): antithrombotic therapy after ACS or PCI in atrial fibrillation

  7. CMonitor closely — Clinically significant: adjust the dose or timing, or monitor a named measure.

    Mobic, Aleve

    2 drugs: risk of acute kidney injury

    Why C: The Warnings and Precautions section of the Mobic label addresses this combination: risk of acute kidney injury. Clinical rulebook row 14.

    Patients at greatest risk of this reaction are those with impaired renal function, dehydration, hypovolemia, heart failure, liver dysfunction, those taking diuretics and ACE inhibitors or ARBs, and the elderly.

    From the Mobic label, Warnings and Precautions section.Open on DailyMed

    Why it happens

    NSAIDs remove the prostaglandins that keep blood flowing into the kidney, ACE inhibitors and ARBs lower the pressure that keeps it filtering, and diuretics reduce blood volume (the "triple whammy"). Aminoglycosides, vancomycin, and other listed drugs injure the kidney directly.

    What to do

    Avoid the NSAID if possible, keep the patient hydrated, and check creatinine within a week, sooner in the hospital.

    FDA labels: Mobic · Aleve · Lapi et al., BMJ 2013: diuretic + ACE inhibitor/ARB + NSAID and acute kidney injury
