  1. DConsider changing therapy — Dangerous: avoid, or use only with a specific reason and close monitoring.

    Celexa, Haldol, Zithromax, Diflucan

    4 QT-prolonging drugs: risk of QT prolongation and torsades de pointes

    Why D: The Contraindications section of the Celexa label addresses this combination: risk of QT prolongation and torsades de pointes. Clinical rulebook row 23.

    4 CONTRAINDICATIONS Citalopram tablets are contraindicated in patients: taking, or within 14 days of stopping, MAOIs (including MAOIs such as linezolid or intravenous methylene blue) because of an increased risk of serotonin syndrome [see Warnings and Precautions (5.3) , Drug Interactions (7) ] . taking pimozide because of risk of QT prolongation [see Drug Interactions (7) ] . with known hypersensitivity to citalopram or any of the inactive ingredients in citalopram tablets.

    From the Celexa label, Contraindications section.Open on DailyMed

    Why it happens

    Each drug slows the heart's electrical recovery (the QT interval). Together the effect adds up and can trigger torsades de pointes, a dangerous heart rhythm.

    What to do

    Get a baseline ECG and keep potassium and magnesium normal. Stop or switch a drug if the QTc passes 500 ms.

    FDA labels: Celexa · Haldol · Zithromax · Diflucan · Dechanont et al., Pharmacoepidemiol Drug Saf 2014: drug interactions in hospital admissions

  2. DConsider changing therapy — Dangerous: avoid, or use only with a specific reason and close monitoring.

    Celexa, Diflucan

    Celexa with Diflucan: citalopram levels rise: dose-related QT prolongation

    Why D: The Warnings and Precautions section of the Celexa label addresses this combination: citalopram levels rise: dose-related QT prolongation. Clinical rulebook row 23.

    The maximum dose should be limited to 20 mg once daily in patients who are CYP2C19 poor metabolizers or those patients receiving concomitant cimetidine or another CYP2C19 inhibitor, since higher citalopram exposures would be expected.

    From the Celexa label, Warnings and Precautions section.Open on DailyMed

    Why it happens

    Citalopram is cleared by CYP2C19 and prolongs QT in a dose-related way. The citalopram label caps the dose at 20 mg a day with CYP2C19 inhibitors.

    What to do

    Keep citalopram at 20 mg a day or less, or switch to an antidepressant that does not prolong QT.

    FDA labels: Celexa · Diflucan

  3. CMonitor closely — Clinically significant: adjust the dose or timing, or monitor a named measure.

    Celexa, Haldol, Zithromax, Diflucan, Lasix

    5 drugs: low potassium and magnesium from the diuretic raise the rhythm risk

    Why C: The Precautions section of the Lasix label addresses this combination: low potassium and magnesium from the diuretic raise the rhythm risk. Clinical rulebook row 18, 23.

    Hypokalemia may develop with furosemide, especially with brisk diuresis, inadequate oral electrolyte intake, when cirrhosis is present, or during concomitant use of corticosteroids, ACTH, licorice in large amounts, or prolonged use of laxatives.

    From the Lasix label, Precautions section.Open on DailyMed

    Why it happens

    Loop and thiazide diuretics lower potassium and magnesium. Low levels make QT-prolonging drugs more likely to cause torsades and make digoxin more toxic.

    What to do

    Check potassium and magnesium at the start and after dose changes; replace them to keep potassium above 4.0 mmol/L.

    FDA labels: Celexa · Haldol · Zithromax · Diflucan · Lasix

  4. CMonitor closely — Clinically significant: adjust the dose or timing, or monitor a named measure.

    Haldol, Diflucan

    Haldol with Diflucan: haloperidol levels rise: more QT and movement side effects

    Why C: The Precautions section of the Haldol label addresses this combination: haloperidol levels rise: more QT and movement side effects. Clinical rulebook row 23.

    Examples include (but are not limited to): Class 1A antiarrhythmics (e.g., procainamide, quinidine, disopyramide); Class 3 antiarrhythmics (e.g., amiodarone, sotalol); and other drugs such as citalopram, erythromycin, levofloxacin, methadone, and ziprasidone.

    From the Haldol label, Precautions section.Open on DailyMed

    Why it happens

    Haloperidol is partly cleared by CYP3A4; inhibitors raise its level, adding QT prolongation and extrapyramidal side effects.

    What to do

    Monitor for stiffness, restlessness, and QT changes; lower the dose if needed.

    FDA labels: Haldol · Diflucan · FDA: Drug Development and Drug Interactions, Table of Substrates, Inhibitors and Inducers

    Why A: These labels each mention this risk, but no class rule applies to this combination.
