Why it happens

Each drug slows the heart's electrical recovery (the QT interval). Together the effect adds up and can trigger torsades de pointes, a dangerous heart rhythm.

What to do

Get a baseline ECG and keep potassium and magnesium normal. Stop or switch a drug if the QTc passes 500 ms.

Why it happens

Each drug blocks acetylcholine. Together they cause confusion or delirium, dry mouth, blurred vision, urinary retention, constipation or ileus, overheating, and falls, worst in older adults (Beers criteria).

What to do

Replace the strongest anticholinergic with an alternative and avoid the combination in older adults.
