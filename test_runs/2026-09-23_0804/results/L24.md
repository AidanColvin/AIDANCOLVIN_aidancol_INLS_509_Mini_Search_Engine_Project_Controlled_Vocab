Mobic, Aleve

2 drugs: risk of serious bleeding, especially stomach and gut bleeding

Why C: Class rule: risk of serious bleeding, especially stomach and gut bleeding. Clinical rulebook row 9, 12, 13.

Why it happens

Anticoagulants slow clotting, aspirin and other antiplatelets stop platelets from sticking, NSAIDs block platelets and injure the stomach lining, and SSRIs and SNRIs drain the serotonin platelets need. Each added drug raises the bleeding risk.

What to do

Drop any drug that is not essential (WOEST and AUGUSTUS support dropping aspirin from triple therapy), add a proton pump inhibitor for stomach protection, and watch for black stools, vomiting blood, or anemia.

Why C: The Warnings and Precautions section of the Mobic label addresses this combination: risk of acute kidney injury. Clinical rulebook row 14.

Patients at greatest risk of this reaction are those with impaired renal function, dehydration, hypovolemia, heart failure, liver dysfunction, those taking diuretics and ACE inhibitors or ARBs, and the elderly.

From the Mobic label, Warnings and Precautions section.
Open on DailyMed

Why it happens

NSAIDs remove the prostaglandins that keep blood flowing into the kidney, ACE inhibitors and ARBs lower the pressure that keeps it filtering, and diuretics reduce blood volume (the "triple whammy"). Aminoglycosides, vancomycin, and other listed drugs injure the kidney directly.

What to do

Avoid the NSAID if possible, keep the patient hydrated, and check creatinine within a week, sooner in the hospital.
