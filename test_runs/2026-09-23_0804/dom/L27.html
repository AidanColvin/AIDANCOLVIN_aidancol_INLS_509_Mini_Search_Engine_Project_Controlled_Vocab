    Azilect, Demerol

    Azilect with Demerol: can cause serotonin syndrome

    Why E: The Contraindications section of the Azilect label addresses this combination: can cause serotonin syndrome. Clinical rulebook row 7.

    4 CONTRAINDICATIONS Rasagiline is contraindicated for use with meperidine, tramadol, methadone, propoxyphene and MAO inhibitors (MAOIs), including other selective MAO-B inhibitors, because of risk of serotonin syndrome [see Warnings and Precautions ( 5.2 )] .

    From the Azilect label, Contraindications section.Open on DailyMed

    Why it happens

    The rasagiline and selegiline labels contraindicate meperidine, tramadol, methadone, dextromethorphan, and similar drugs because serious, sometimes fatal serotonin syndrome has occurred.

    What to do

    Do not use together; wait at least 14 days after stopping the MAO-B inhibitor.

    FDA labels: Azilect · Demerol · Dunkley et al., QJM 2003: the Hunter serotonin toxicity criteria

  2. DConsider changing therapy — Dangerous: avoid, or use only with a specific reason and close monitoring.

    Haldol, Demerol

    2 CNS depressants: risk of oversedation, slowed breathing (respiratory depression), falls, and overdose

    Why D: The Boxed Warning section of the Demerol label addresses this combination: risk of oversedation, slowed breathing (respiratory depression), falls, and overdose. Clinical rulebook row 1, 2.

    Risks from Concomitant Use with Benzodiazepines or Other CNS Depressants Concomitant use of opioids with benzodiazepines or other central nervous system (CNS) depressants, including alcohol, may result in profound sedation, respiratory depression, coma, and death.

    From the Demerol label, Boxed Warning section.Open on DailyMed

    Why it happens

    Each drug depresses the brain on its own; together the effects add up. Opioids with benzodiazepines, gabapentinoids, or other sedatives can stop breathing: both opioid and benzodiazepine labels carry a boxed warning on this combination.

    What to do

    Use the lowest doses for the shortest time, avoid adding more sedatives, and watch for sleepiness and slow or shallow breathing. With an opioid, co-prescribe naloxone.

    FDA labels: Haldol · Demerol · Phansalkar et al., JAMIA 2012: high-priority drug-drug interactions (ONC expert panel)

  3. DConsider changing therapy — Dangerous: avoid, or use only with a specific reason and close monitoring.

    Azilect, Reglan, Demerol, Lexapro

    4 serotonergic drugs: risk of serotonin syndrome

    Why D: The Contraindications section of the Lexapro label addresses this combination: risk of serotonin syndrome. Clinical rulebook row 7.

    4 CONTRAINDICATIONS Escitalopram tablets is contraindicated in patients: taking MAOIs with Escitalopram tablets or within 14 days of stopping treatment with Escitalopram tablets because of an increased risk of serotonin syndrome.

    From the Lexapro label, Contraindications section.Open on DailyMed

    Why it happens

    Each drug raises serotonin activity. Together they can cause serotonin syndrome: agitation, tremor, clonus and overactive reflexes, sweating, fever, fast heart rate.

    What to do

    Avoid stacking serotonergic drugs where possible; if needed, use low doses and watch for clonus, tremor, fever, and agitation, especially in the first days after a start or dose increase.

    FDA labels: Azilect · Reglan · Demerol · Lexapro · Dunkley et al., QJM 2003: the Hunter serotonin toxicity criteria

    Sinemet, Reglan, Haldol

    Sinemet with 2 drugs that block dopamine: blocks levodopa and worsens parkinsonism

    Why D: The Drug Interactions section of the Reglan label addresses this combination: blocks levodopa and worsens parkinsonism.

    Examples apomorphine, bromocriptine, cabergoline, levodopa, pramipexole, ropinirole, and rotigotine 7.2 Effects of Metoclopramide on Other Drugs Table 4 displays the effects of metoclopramide on other drugs.

    From the Reglan label, Drug Interactions section.Open on DailyMed

    Why it happens

    These drugs block dopamine D2 receptors, directly opposing levodopa and worsening Parkinson's symptoms. Metoclopramide's label warns against use in Parkinson's disease.

    What to do

    Avoid; for nausea use domperidone where available or ondansetron, and if an antipsychotic is needed use quetiapine, clozapine, or pimavanserin.

    FDA labels: Sinemet · Reglan · Haldol

  5. DConsider changing therapy — Dangerous: avoid, or use only with a specific reason and close monitoring.

    Reglan, Haldol

    2 dopamine-blocking drugs: duplicate therapy: two dopamine blockers add movement side effects, tardive dyskinesia, and NMS risk

    Why D: Class rule: duplicate therapy: two dopamine blockers add movement side effects, tardive dyskinesia, and NMS risk.

    Why it happens

    These are all dopamine-blocking drugs: taking more than one is therapeutic duplication.

    What to do

    Avoid combining metoclopramide with an antipsychotic.

    FDA labels: Reglan · Haldol

  6. CMonitor closely — Clinically significant: adjust the dose or timing, or monitor a named measure.

    Haldol, Lexapro

    2 QT-prolonging drugs: risk of QT prolongation and torsades de pointes

    Why C: The Warnings section of the Haldol label addresses this combination: risk of QT prolongation and torsades de pointes. Clinical rulebook row 23.

    Although cases have been reported even in the absence of predisposing factors, particular caution is advised in treating patients with other QT-prolonging conditions (including electrolyte imbalance [particularly hypokalemia and hypomagnesemia], drugs known to prolong QT, underlying cardiac abnormalities, hypothyroidism, and familial long QT-syndrome).

    From the Haldol label, Warnings section.Open on DailyMed

    Why it happens

    Each drug slows the heart's electrical recovery (the QT interval). Together the effect adds up and can trigger torsades de pointes, a dangerous heart rhythm.

    What to do

    Get a baseline ECG and keep potassium and magnesium normal. Stop or switch a drug if the QTc passes 500 ms.

    FDA labels: Haldol · Lexapro · Dechanont et al., Pharmacoepidemiol Drug Saf 2014: drug interactions in hospital admissions
