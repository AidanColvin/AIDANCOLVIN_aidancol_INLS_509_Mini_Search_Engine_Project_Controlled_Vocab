  1. DConsider changing therapy — Dangerous: avoid, or use only with a specific reason and close monitoring.

    Risperdal, Invega, Zyprexa, Klonopin

    4 CNS depressants: risk of oversedation, slowed breathing (respiratory depression), falls, and overdose

    Why D: Class rule: risk of oversedation, slowed breathing (respiratory depression), falls, and overdose. Clinical rulebook row 1, 2.

    Why it happens

    Each drug depresses the brain on its own; together the effects add up. Opioids with benzodiazepines, gabapentinoids, or other sedatives can stop breathing: both opioid and benzodiazepine labels carry a boxed warning on this combination.

    What to do

    Use the lowest doses for the shortest time, avoid adding more sedatives, and watch for sleepiness and slow or shallow breathing. With an opioid, co-prescribe naloxone.

    FDA labels: Risperdal · Invega · Zyprexa · Klonopin · Phansalkar et al., JAMIA 2012: high-priority drug-drug interactions (ONC expert panel)

  2. DConsider changing therapy — Dangerous: avoid, or use only with a specific reason and close monitoring.

    3 antipsychotics: duplicate therapy: more movement side effects, QT, prolactin, and metabolic effects

    Why D: Class rule: duplicate therapy: more movement side effects, QT, prolactin, and metabolic effects.

    Why it happens

    These are all antipsychotics: taking more than one is therapeutic duplication.

    What to do

    Use one antipsychotic unless a psychiatrist is deliberately combining them.

    FDA labels: Risperdal · Invega · Zyprexa

    Risperdal, Invega

    Duplicate: Risperdal (risperidone) and Invega (paliperidone): one is the active metabolite of the other: combined paliperidone 6 mg/day and risperidone 4 mg/day

    Why D: Hidden duplication: the entries name one drug differently, so the doses add up unnoticed.

    Why it happens

    Both entries deliver the same active drug, so the patient gets the combined dose. One is the active metabolite of the other.

    What to do

    Confirm whether both lines are meant. If one is a duplicate entry, remove it; if both are real, treat the combined total as the dose.

    FDA labels: Risperdal · Invega

  4. CMonitor closely — Clinically significant: adjust the dose or timing, or monitor a named measure.

    Risperdal, Invega

    2 QT-prolonging drugs: risk of QT prolongation and torsades de pointes

    Why C: The Warnings and Precautions section of the Invega label addresses this combination: risk of QT prolongation and torsades de pointes. Clinical rulebook row 23.

    Certain circumstances may increase the risk of the occurrence of torsade de pointes and/or sudden death in association with the use of drugs that prolong the QTc interval, including (1) bradycardia; (2) hypokalemia or hypomagnesemia; (3) concomitant use of other drugs that prolong the QTc interval; and (4) presence of congenital prolongation of the QT interval.

    From the Invega label, Warnings and Precautions section.Open on DailyMed

    Why it happens

    Each drug slows the heart's electrical recovery (the QT interval). Together the effect adds up and can trigger torsades de pointes, a dangerous heart rhythm.

    What to do

    Get a baseline ECG and keep potassium and magnesium normal. Stop or switch a drug if the QTc passes 500 ms.

    FDA labels: Risperdal · Invega · Dechanont et al., Pharmacoepidemiol Drug Saf 2014: drug interactions in hospital admissions

  5. CMonitor closely — Clinically significant: adjust the dose or timing, or monitor a named measure.

    Zyprexa, Cogentin

    2 anticholinergic drugs: anticholinergic burden: confusion, urinary retention, constipation, falls

    Why C: The Warnings and Precautions section of the Zyprexa label addresses this combination: anticholinergic burden: confusion, urinary retention, constipation, falls. Clinical rulebook row 6.

    ( 5.12 ) • Anticholinergic (antimuscarinic) Effects: Use with caution with other anticholinergic drugs and in patients with urinary retention, prostatic hypertrophy, constipation, paralytic ileus or related conditions.

    From the Zyprexa label, Warnings and Precautions section.Open on DailyMed

    Why it happens

    Each drug blocks acetylcholine. Together they cause confusion or delirium, dry mouth, blurred vision, urinary retention, constipation or ileus, overheating, and falls, worst in older adults (Beers criteria).

    What to do

    Replace the strongest anticholinergic with an alternative and avoid the combination in older adults.

    FDA labels: Zyprexa · Cogentin
