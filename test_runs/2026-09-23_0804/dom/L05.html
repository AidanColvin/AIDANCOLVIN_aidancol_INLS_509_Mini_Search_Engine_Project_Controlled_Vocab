    Table 1: Amiodarone Drug Interactions Concomitant Drug Class/Name Examples Clinical Comment Pharmacodynamic Interactions QT Prolonging Drugs class I and III antiarrhythmics, lithium, certain phenothiazines, tricyclic antidepressants, certain fluoroquinolone and macrolide antibiotics, azole antifungals, halogenated inhalation anesthetic agents Increased risk of Torsade de Pointes.

    From the Cordarone label, Drug Interactions section.Open on DailyMed

    Why it happens

    Each drug slows the heart's electrical recovery (the QT interval). Together the effect adds up and can trigger torsades de pointes, a dangerous heart rhythm.

    What to do

    Get a baseline ECG and keep potassium and magnesium normal. Stop or switch a drug if the QTc passes 500 ms.

    FDA labels: Cordarone · Lexapro · Dechanont et al., Pharmacoepidemiol Drug Saf 2014: drug interactions in hospital admissions

  2. DConsider changing therapy — Dangerous: avoid, or use only with a specific reason and close monitoring.

    Coumadin, Lexapro, Bayer, Plavix

    4 drugs: risk of serious bleeding, especially stomach and gut bleeding

    Why D: The Warnings and Precautions section of the Coumadin label addresses this combination: risk of serious bleeding, especially stomach and gut bleeding. Clinical rulebook row 9, 12, 13.

    Risk factors for bleeding include high intensity of anticoagulation (INR >4.0), age greater than or equal to 65, history of highly variable INRs, history of gastrointestinal bleeding, hypertension, cerebrovascular disease, anemia, malignancy, trauma, renal impairment, certain genetic factors [see Clinical Pharmacology (12.5) ] , certain concomitant drugs [see Drug Interactions (7) ] , and long duration of warfarin therapy.

    From the Coumadin label, Warnings and Precautions section.Open on DailyMed

    Why it happens

    Anticoagulants slow clotting, aspirin and other antiplatelets stop platelets from sticking, NSAIDs block platelets and injure the stomach lining, and SSRIs and SNRIs drain the serotonin platelets need. Each added drug raises the bleeding risk.

    What to do

    Drop any drug that is not essential (WOEST and AUGUSTUS support dropping aspirin from triple therapy), add a proton pump inhibitor for stomach protection, and watch for black stools, vomiting blood, or anemia.

    FDA labels: Coumadin · Lexapro · Bayer · Plavix · Dechanont et al., Pharmacoepidemiol Drug Saf 2014: drug interactions in hospital admissions · Dewilde et al., Lancet 2013 (WOEST): dropping aspirin from triple therapy · Lopes et al., NEJM 2019 (AUGUSTUS): antithrombotic therapy after ACS or PCI in atrial fibrillation

  3. DConsider changing therapy — Dangerous: avoid, or use only with a specific reason and close monitoring.

    Coumadin, Bactrim DS, Cordarone

    Coumadin with 2 drugs that raise the INR: INR rises: risk of serious bleeding

    Why D: Class rule: INR rises: risk of serious bleeding. Clinical rulebook row 10.

    Why it happens

    These drugs block CYP2C9, which clears S-warfarin (the more potent half of warfarin). The INR can double within days; amiodarone's effect lasts for weeks after it stops.

    What to do

    Choose another drug if possible; otherwise lower the warfarin dose (often 25 to 50%) and check the INR within 3 to 5 days.

    FDA labels: Coumadin · Bactrim DS · Cordarone · Phansalkar et al., JAMIA 2012: high-priority drug-drug interactions (ONC expert panel)
