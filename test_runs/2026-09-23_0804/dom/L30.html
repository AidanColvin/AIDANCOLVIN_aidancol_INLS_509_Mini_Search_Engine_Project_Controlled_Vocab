  1. DConsider changing therapy — Dangerous: avoid, or use only with a specific reason and close monitoring.

    Prilosec, Plavix

    Plavix with Prilosec: clopidogrel may not work as well

    Why D: The Warnings and Precautions section of the Plavix label addresses this combination: clopidogrel may not work as well. Clinical rulebook row 31.

    5 WARNINGS AND PRECAUTIONS • CYP2C19 inhibitors: Avoid concomitant use of omeprazole or esomeprazole.

    From the Plavix label, Warnings and Precautions section.Open on DailyMed

    Why it happens

    Clopidogrel is a prodrug activated by CYP2C19. Omeprazole and esomeprazole inhibit CYP2C19 and cut the active metabolite (efficacy loss); the clopidogrel label says to avoid them. The clinical harm is contested: the COGENT trial found no increase in cardiac events.

    What to do

    Use pantoprazole or famotidine if stomach protection is needed.

    FDA labels: Prilosec · Plavix · Bhatt et al., NEJM 2010 (COGENT): clopidogrel with or without omeprazole

  2. DConsider changing therapy — Dangerous: avoid, or use only with a specific reason and close monitoring.

    Tums Ultra, Cipro, Feosol

    Cipro with 2 mineral products that bind it: the antibiotic is not absorbed and may fail

    Why D: The Drug Interactions section of the Cipro label addresses this combination: the antibiotic is not absorbed and may fail.

    Monitor serum creatinine ( 7 ) Multivalent cation-containing products including antacids, metal cations or didanosine Decreased ciprofloxacin absorption.

    From the Cipro label, Drug Interactions section.Open on DailyMed

    Why it happens

    Calcium, iron, magnesium, aluminum, and zinc bind (chelate) the antibiotic in the gut; ciprofloxacin absorption falls by about 40 to 50%.

    What to do

    Separate the doses: give the antibiotic at least 2 hours before or 6 hours after the mineral product, or pause the supplement during the course.

    FDA labels: Tums Ultra · Cipro · Feosol

  3. CMonitor closely — Clinically significant: adjust the dose or timing, or monitor a named measure.

    Synthroid, Tums Ultra, Feosol

    Synthroid with 2 drugs that bind levothyroxine: less levothyroxine is absorbed

    Why C: The Warnings and Precautions section of the Synthroid label addresses this combination: less levothyroxine is absorbed.

    The increased bone resorption may be associated with increased serum levels and urinary excretion of calcium and phosphorous, elevations in bone alkaline phosphatase, and suppressed serum parathyroid hormone levels.

    From the Synthroid label, Warnings and Precautions section.Open on DailyMed

    Why it happens

    Calcium, iron, and binders hold levothyroxine in the gut and reduce its absorption.

    What to do

    Separate the doses by at least 4 hours and recheck TSH in 6 to 8 weeks.

    FDA labels: Synthroid · Tums Ultra · Feosol

    Cipro, Prilosec

    QT Prolongation Risk shared by 2 drugs

    Why A: These labels each mention this risk, but no class rule applies to this combination.

    QT Prolongation: Prolongation of the QT interval and isolated cases of torsade de pointes have been reported.

    From the Cipro label, Warnings and Precautions section.Open on DailyMed

    What this means: QT prolongation

    Each of these drugs can delay the heart's electrical recovery after a beat, seen as a longer QT interval on an ECG. Together the delay can add up and trigger torsades de pointes, a dangerous heart rhythm.

    Source: National Heart, Lung, and Blood Institute, NIH
