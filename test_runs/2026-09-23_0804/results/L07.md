Nardil, Demerol

2 serotonergic drugs: risk of serotonin syndrome

Why D: Class rule: risk of serotonin syndrome. Clinical rulebook row 7.

Why it happens

Each drug raises serotonin activity. Together they can cause serotonin syndrome: agitation, tremor, clonus and overactive reflexes, sweating, fever, fast heart rate.

What to do

Avoid stacking serotonergic drugs where possible; if needed, use low doses and watch for clonus, tremor, fever, and agitation, especially in the first days after a start or dose increase.

Why C: The Contraindications section of the Nardil label addresses this combination: risk of low blood pressure on standing, fainting, and falls. Clinical rulebook row 22.

A List of MAO Inhibitors by Generic Name Follows: pargyline hydrochloride pargyline hydrochloride and methylclothiazide furazolidone isocarboxazid procarbazine tranylcypromine Phenelzine sulfate should also not be used in combination with buspirone HCl, since several cases of elevated blood pressure have been reported in patients taking MAO inhibitors who were then given buspirone HCl.

Why it happens

Each drug lowers blood pressure or blocks the reflex that holds it up when standing. Together they can cause orthostatic hypotension, fainting, and falls.

What to do

Start low, raise one drug at a time, check standing blood pressure, and tell the patient to stand up slowly.
