    Why it happens

    Latuda, Dolophine

    2 CNS depressants: risk of oversedation, slowed breathing (respiratory depression), falls, and overdose

    Why D: The Boxed Warning section of the Dolophine label addresses this combination: risk of oversedation, slowed breathing (respiratory depression), falls, and overdose. Clinical rulebook row 1, 2.

    Risks From Concomitant Use With Benzodiazepines Or Other CNS Depressants Concomitant use of opioids with benzodiazepines or other central nervous system (CNS) depressants, including alcohol, may result in profound sedation, respiratory depression, coma, and death.

    From the Dolophine label, Boxed Warning section.Open on DailyMed

    Why it happens

    Each drug depresses the brain on its own; together the effects add up. Opioids with benzodiazepines, gabapentinoids, or other sedatives can stop breathing: both opioid and benzodiazepine labels carry a boxed warning on this combination.

    What to do

    Use the lowest doses for the shortest time, avoid adding more sedatives, and watch for sleepiness and slow or shallow breathing. With an opioid, co-prescribe naloxone.

    FDA labels: Latuda · Dolophine · Phansalkar et al., JAMIA 2012: high-priority drug-drug interactions (ONC expert panel)

    Prograf, Dolophine

    2 QT-prolonging drugs: risk of QT prolongation and torsades de pointes

    Why D: The Warnings and Precautions section of the Prograf label addresses this combination: risk of QT prolongation and torsades de pointes. Clinical rulebook row 23.

    Closely monitor patients with risk factors for development of prolonged QT interval, a history of cardiac conduction abnormalities, and those taking medications affecting cardiac conduction for changes in cardiac rhythm during initiation and titration of methadone hydrochloride tablets [see Warnings and Precautions (5.4) ].

    From the Dolophine label, Boxed Warning section.Open on DailyMed

    Why it happens

    Each drug slows the heart's electrical recovery (the QT interval). Together the effect adds up and can trigger torsades de pointes, a dangerous heart rhythm.

    What to do

    Get a baseline ECG and keep potassium and magnesium normal. Stop or switch a drug if the QTc passes 500 ms.

    FDA labels: Prograf · Dolophine · Dechanont et al., Pharmacoepidemiol Drug Saf 2014: drug interactions in hospital admissions

    Rifadin, Prograf

    Prograf with Rifadin: immunosuppressant levels fall: risk of rejection

    Why D: The Warnings and Precautions section of the Prograf label addresses this combination: immunosuppressant levels fall: risk of rejection. Clinical rulebook row 33.

    5.11 Interactions with CYP3A4 Inhibitors and Inducers When co-administering tacrolimus with strong CYP3A4 inhibitors (e.g., telaprevir, boceprevir, ritonavir, ketoconazole, itraconazole, voriconazole, clarithromycin) and strong inducers (e.g., rifampin, rifabutin), adjustments in the dosing regimen of tacrolimus and subsequent frequent monitoring of tacrolimus whole blood trough concentrations and tacrolimus-associated adverse reactions are recommended.

    From the Prograf label, Warnings and Precautions section.Open on DailyMed

    Why it happens

    Enzyme inducers speed tacrolimus and cyclosporine clearance, so levels fall sharply within 1 to 2 weeks (efficacy loss) and graft rejection can follow.

    What to do

    Avoid if possible; otherwise raise the dose and follow trough levels closely, including after the inducer stops.

    FDA labels: Rifadin · Prograf · FDA: Drug Development and Drug Interactions, Table of Substrates, Inhibitors and Inducers

    Rifadin, Xarelto

    Xarelto with Rifadin: anticoagulant levels fall: risk of stroke or clot

    Why D: The Warnings and Precautions section of the Xarelto label addresses this combination: anticoagulant levels fall: risk of stroke or clot. Clinical rulebook row 33.

    Avoid concomitant use of XARELTO with drugs that are known combined P-gp and strong CYP3A inducers [see Drug Interactions (7.3) ] .

    From the Xarelto label, Warnings and Precautions section.Open on DailyMed

    Why it happens

    Rifampin, carbamazepine, phenytoin, and St. John's wort induce CYP3A4 and P-glycoprotein and roughly halve DOAC exposure, so the anticoagulant can fail (efficacy loss).

    What to do

    Avoid; use another anticoagulant (such as warfarin with INR monitoring) while the inducer is needed.

    FDA labels: Rifadin · Xarelto

    Rifadin, Dolophine

    Dolophine with Rifadin: opioid levels fall: withdrawal or loss of pain control

    Why D: The Drug Interactions section of the Dolophine label addresses this combination: opioid levels fall: withdrawal or loss of pain control. Clinical rulebook row 33.

    Examples: Rifampin, carbamazepine, phenytoin, St.

    From the Dolophine label, Drug Interactions section.Open on DailyMed

    Why it happens

    Rifampin, carbamazepine, and phenytoin induce CYP3A4 and cut opioid levels within days (efficacy loss), causing withdrawal; stopping the inducer later can cause overdose.

    What to do

    Raise the opioid dose under close monitoring, and lower it again when the inducer stops.
