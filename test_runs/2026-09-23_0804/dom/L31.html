  1. DConsider changing therapy — Dangerous: avoid, or use only with a specific reason and close monitoring.

    Vancocin, Garamycin, Lasix, Toradol, Zestril, Zosyn

    6 drugs: risk of acute kidney injury

    Why D: The Warnings section of the Toradol label addresses this combination: risk of acute kidney injury. Clinical rulebook row 14.

    Patients at greatest risk of this reaction are those with impaired renal function, heart failure, liver dysfunction, those taking diuretics and ACE inhibitors, and the elderly.

    From the Toradol label, Warnings section.Open on DailyMed

    Why it happens

    NSAIDs remove the prostaglandins that keep blood flowing into the kidney, ACE inhibitors and ARBs lower the pressure that keeps it filtering, and diuretics reduce blood volume (the "triple whammy"). Aminoglycosides, vancomycin, and other listed drugs injure the kidney directly.

    What to do

    Avoid the NSAID if possible, keep the patient hydrated, and check creatinine within a week, sooner in the hospital.

    FDA labels: Vancocin · Garamycin · Lasix · Toradol · Zestril · Zosyn · Lapi et al., BMJ 2013: diuretic + ACE inhibitor/ARB + NSAID and acute kidney injury

  2. DConsider changing therapy — Dangerous: avoid, or use only with a specific reason and close monitoring.

    Garamycin, Lasix

    Garamycin with Lasix: additive, sometimes permanent hearing loss

    Why D: The Boxed Warning section of the Garamycin label addresses this combination: additive, sometimes permanent hearing loss.

    The concurrent use of gentamicin with potent diuretics, such as ethacrynic acid or furosemide, should be avoided, since certain diuretics by themselves may cause ototoxicity.

    From the Garamycin label, Boxed Warning section.Open on DailyMed

    Why it happens

    Aminoglycosides and loop diuretics both damage the inner ear; together hearing loss can be permanent. Aminoglycoside labels carry a boxed warning against potent diuretics.

    What to do

    Avoid if possible; monitor aminoglycoside levels, kidney function, and hearing.

    FDA labels: Garamycin · Lasix

  3. CMonitor closely — Clinically significant: adjust the dose or timing, or monitor a named measure.

    Toradol, Zestril

    2 drugs: risk of high potassium (hyperkalemia)

    Why C: Class rule: risk of high potassium (hyperkalemia). Clinical rulebook row 15.

    Why it happens

    ACE inhibitors and ARBs lower aldosterone, spironolactone and eplerenone block it, trimethoprim blocks the kidney's potassium channel (like amiloride), and supplements add potassium. Together potassium can rise to levels that stop the heart.

    What to do

    Check potassium and kidney function within 3 days and again at 1 week; stop the potassium supplement or change the antibiotic when possible.

    FDA labels: Toradol · Zestril · Juurlink et al., NEJM 2004: hyperkalemia after spironolactone use rose after RALES · Antoniou et al., CMAJ 2015: trimethoprim-sulfamethoxazole with spironolactone and sudden death
