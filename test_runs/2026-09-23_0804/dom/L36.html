  1. EAvoid combination — Do not use these drugs together.

    Suboxone, Revia

    Suboxone with Revia: the antagonist blocks the opioid and can bring on withdrawal

    Why E: The Contraindications section of the Revia label addresses this combination: the antagonist blocks the opioid and can bring on withdrawal.

    CONTRAINDICATIONS Naltrexone hydrochloride is contraindicated in: Patients receiving opioid analgesics.

    From the Revia label, Contraindications section.Open on DailyMed

    Why it happens

    Naltrexone occupies opioid receptors: it blocks the opioid's effect and precipitates acute withdrawal in a patient who depends on the opioid. The naltrexone label contraindicates use in patients taking opioids. One of these prescriptions is likely an error.

    What to do

    Do not use together. Decide which treatment is intended; naltrexone needs 7 to 10 opioid-free days first.

    FDA labels: Suboxone · Revia

  2. DConsider changing therapy — Dangerous: avoid, or use only with a specific reason and close monitoring.

    Suboxone, Klonopin, Neurontin, Seroquel

    4 CNS depressants: risk of oversedation, slowed breathing (respiratory depression), falls, and overdose

    Why D: The Warnings and Precautions section of the Suboxone label addresses this combination: risk of oversedation, slowed breathing (respiratory depression), falls, and overdose. Clinical rulebook row 1, 2.

    WARNING: RISKS FROM CONCOMITANT USE WITH OPIOIDS; ABUSE, MISUSE, AND ADDICTION; and DEPENDENCE AND WITHDRAWAL REACTIONS Concomitant use of benzodiazepines and opioids may result in profound sedation, respiratory depression, coma, and death.

    From the Klonopin label, Boxed Warning section.Open on DailyMed

    Why it happens

    Each drug depresses the brain on its own; together the effects add up. Opioids with benzodiazepines, gabapentinoids, or other sedatives can stop breathing: both opioid and benzodiazepine labels carry a boxed warning on this combination.

    What to do

    Use the lowest doses for the shortest time, avoid adding more sedatives, and watch for sleepiness and slow or shallow breathing. With an opioid, co-prescribe naloxone.

    • Suboxone with Klonopin: boxed warning: profound sedation, respiratory depression, coma, death. Opioids and benzodiazepines both suppress the brainstem's drive to breathe. Both drug classes carry an FDA boxed warning against this combination.
    • Suboxone with Neurontin: risk of respiratory depression. Gabapentin and pregabalin add to opioid respiratory depression; the FDA added this warning to both in 2019. Both are cleared by the kidney, so kidney injury raises exposure.

    FDA labels: Suboxone · Klonopin · Neurontin · Seroquel · Phansalkar et al., JAMIA 2012: high-priority drug-drug interactions (ONC expert panel)

  3. AMinor — Minor: usually no action needed.

    Suboxone, Seroquel

    QT Prolongation Risk shared by 2 drugs

    Why A: These labels each mention this risk, but no class rule applies to this combination.

    5.14 QTc Prolongation Thorough QT studies with buprenorphine products have demonstrated QT prolongation ≤15 msec.

    From the Suboxone label, Warnings and Precautions section.Open on DailyMed

    What this means: QT prolongation

    Each of these drugs can delay the heart's electrical recovery after a beat, seen as a longer QT interval on an ECG. Together the delay can add up and trigger torsades de pointes, a dangerous heart rhythm.

    Source: National Heart, Lung, and Blood Institute, NIH

    FDA labels: Suboxone · Seroquel
