  1. DConsider changing therapy — Dangerous: avoid, or use only with a specific reason and close monitoring.

    Zestril, Microzide, Motrin

    3 drugs: risk of acute kidney injury

    Why D: The Warnings section of the Motrin label addresses this combination: risk of acute kidney injury. Clinical rulebook row 14.

    Patients at greatest risk of this reaction are those with impaired renal function, heart failure, liver dysfunction, those taking diuretics and ACE inhibitors, and the elderly.

    From the Motrin label, Warnings section.Open on DailyMed

    Why it happens

    NSAIDs remove the prostaglandins that keep blood flowing into the kidney, ACE inhibitors and ARBs lower the pressure that keeps it filtering, and diuretics reduce blood volume (the "triple whammy"). Aminoglycosides, vancomycin, and other listed drugs injure the kidney directly.

    What to do

    Avoid the NSAID if possible, keep the patient hydrated, and check creatinine within a week, sooner in the hospital.

    FDA labels: Zestril · Microzide · Motrin · Lapi et al., BMJ 2013: diuretic + ACE inhibitor/ARB + NSAID and acute kidney injury

  2. DConsider changing therapy — Dangerous: avoid, or use only with a specific reason and close monitoring.

    Lithobid, Zestril, Microzide, Motrin

    Lithobid with 3 drugs that raise lithium levels: lithium levels rise: lithium toxicity

    Why D: The Precautions section of the Lithobid label addresses this combination: lithium levels rise: lithium toxicity. Clinical rulebook row 16.

    Drug Interactions Diuretic-, ACE-, and ARB-induced sodium loss may increase serum lithium concentrations.

    From the Lithobid label, Precautions section.Open on DailyMed

    Why it happens

    The kidney handles lithium like sodium. Thiazides (levels up 25 to 40%), ACE inhibitors and ARBs, and NSAIDs all make the kidney hold on to lithium. Toxicity brings coarse tremor, unsteadiness, confusion, and arrhythmia.

    What to do

    Avoid if possible; otherwise lower the lithium dose and check a lithium level within 5 to 7 days (therapeutic range 0.6 to 1.2 mEq/L), plus kidney function and sodium.

    FDA labels: Lithobid · Zestril · Microzide · Motrin · Phansalkar et al., JAMIA 2012: high-priority drug-drug interactions (ONC expert panel)

  3. CMonitor closely — Clinically significant: adjust the dose or timing, or monitor a named measure.

    Zestril, Motrin

    2 drugs: risk of high potassium (hyperkalemia)

    Why C: Class rule: risk of high potassium (hyperkalemia). Clinical rulebook row 15.

    Why it happens

    ACE inhibitors and ARBs lower aldosterone, spironolactone and eplerenone block it, trimethoprim blocks the kidney's potassium channel (like amiloride), and supplements add potassium. Together potassium can rise to levels that stop the heart.

    What to do

    Check potassium and kidney function within 3 days and again at 1 week; stop the potassium supplement or change the antibiotic when possible.

    FDA labels: Zestril · Motrin · Juurlink et al., NEJM 2004: hyperkalemia after spironolactone use rose after RALES · Antoniou et al., CMAJ 2015: trimethoprim-sulfamethoxazole with spironolactone and sudden death

  4. CMonitor closely — Clinically significant: adjust the dose or timing, or monitor a named measure.

    Topamax, Glucophage

    Glucophage with Topamax: risk of metabolic or lactic acidosis

    Why C: Class rule: risk of metabolic or lactic acidosis. Clinical rulebook row 19.

    Why it happens

    Topiramate and other carbonic anhydrase inhibitors cause a metabolic acidosis that adds to metformin's rare lactic acidosis risk; the topiramate label warns about this.

    What to do

    Check a bicarbonate level; stop metformin if acidosis develops or kidney function falls.

    FDA labels: Topamax · Glucophage

    From the Topamax label, Drug Interactions section.Open on DailyMed
