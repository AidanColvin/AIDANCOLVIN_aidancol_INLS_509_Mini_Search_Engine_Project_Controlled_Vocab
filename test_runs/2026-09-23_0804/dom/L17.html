  1. DConsider changing therapy — Dangerous: avoid, or use only with a specific reason and close monitoring.

    Bactrim DS, Aldactone, Cozaar, Klor-Con, Celebrex

    5 drugs: risk of high potassium (hyperkalemia)

    Why D: The Contraindications section of the Aldactone label addresses this combination: risk of high potassium (hyperkalemia). Clinical rulebook row 15.

    4 CONTRAINDICATIONS Spironolactone tablets are contraindicated in the patients with: Hyperkalemia Addison's disease Concomitant use of eplerenone Spironolactone tablets are contraindicated in patients with ( 4 ): Hyperkalemia Addison's disease Concomitant use of eplerenone

    From the Aldactone label, Contraindications section.Open on DailyMed

    Why it happens

    ACE inhibitors and ARBs lower aldosterone, spironolactone and eplerenone block it, trimethoprim blocks the kidney's potassium channel (like amiloride), and supplements add potassium. Together potassium can rise to levels that stop the heart.

    What to do

    Check potassium and kidney function within 3 days and again at 1 week; stop the potassium supplement or change the antibiotic when possible.

    FDA labels: Bactrim DS · Aldactone · Cozaar · Klor-Con · Celebrex · Juurlink et al., NEJM 2004: hyperkalemia after spironolactone use rose after RALES · Antoniou et al., CMAJ 2015: trimethoprim-sulfamethoxazole with spironolactone and sudden death

  2. DConsider changing therapy — Dangerous: avoid, or use only with a specific reason and close monitoring.

    Aldactone, Cozaar, Celebrex, Jardiance

    4 drugs: risk of acute kidney injury

    Why D: The Warnings and Precautions section of the Celebrex label addresses this combination: risk of acute kidney injury. Clinical rulebook row 14.

    Patients at greatest risk of this reaction are those with impaired renal function, dehydration, hypovolemia , heart failure, liver dysfunction, those taking diuretics, ACE inhibitors or the ARBs, and the elderly.

    From the Celebrex label, Warnings and Precautions section.Open on DailyMed

    Why it happens

    NSAIDs remove the prostaglandins that keep blood flowing into the kidney, ACE inhibitors and ARBs lower the pressure that keeps it filtering, and diuretics reduce blood volume (the "triple whammy"). Aminoglycosides, vancomycin, and other listed drugs injure the kidney directly.

    What to do

    Avoid the NSAID if possible, keep the patient hydrated, and check creatinine within a week, sooner in the hospital.

    FDA labels: Aldactone · Cozaar · Celebrex · Jardiance · Lapi et al., BMJ 2013: diuretic + ACE inhibitor/ARB + NSAID and acute kidney injury
