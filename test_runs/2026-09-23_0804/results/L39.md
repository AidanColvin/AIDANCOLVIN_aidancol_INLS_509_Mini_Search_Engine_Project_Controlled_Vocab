Ery-Tab, Zocor

Zocor with Ery-Tab: statin levels rise many-fold: risk of rhabdomyolysis

Why E: The Contraindications section of the Zocor label addresses this combination: statin levels rise many-fold: risk of rhabdomyolysis. Clinical rulebook row 27.

4 CONTRAINDICATIONS Simvastatin is contraindicated in the following conditions: • Concomitant use of strong CYP3A4 inhibitors (select azole anti-fungals, macrolide antibiotics, anti-viral medications, and nefazodone) [see DRUG INTERACTIONS ( 7.1 )]. • Concomitant use of cyclosporine, danazol or gemfibrozil [see DRUG INTERACTIONS ( 7.1 )]. • Acute liver failure or decompensated cirrhosis [see WARNINGS AND PRECAUTIONS ( 5.3 )] • Hypersensitivity to simvastatin or any excipients in simvastatin tablets USP.

From the Zocor label, Contraindications section.
Open on DailyMed

Why it happens

Simvastatin and lovastatin are cleared almost entirely by CYP3A4. Strong CYP3A4 inhibitors (and erythromycin, cyclosporine, gemfibrozil, danazol) raise their levels roughly 10-fold, causing muscle breakdown and kidney failure. The simvastatin label contraindicates these combinations.

What to do

Do not use together. Hold the statin during the antibiotic or antifungal course, or switch to pravastatin or rosuvastatin.

Why D: The Warnings and Precautions section of the Zocor label addresses this combination: raises the statin level: risk of muscle injury. Clinical rulebook row 27.

Why it happens

These drugs slow simvastatin's clearance. The simvastatin label caps the dose at 10 mg a day with verapamil, diltiazem, or dronedarone, and 20 mg with amiodarone, amlodipine, or ranolazine.

What to do

Keep simvastatin at or below the label's cap, or switch to a statin not cleared by CYP3A4 (pravastatin, rosuvastatin).

Colcrys, Ery-Tab

Colcrys with Ery-Tab: colchicine levels rise: risk of fatal colchicine toxicity

Why D: The Contraindications section of the Colcrys label addresses this combination: colchicine levels rise: risk of fatal colchicine toxicity. Clinical rulebook row 28.

Patients with renal or hepatic impairment should not be given colchicine tablets in conjunction with P-gp or strong CYP3A4 inhibitors (this includes all protease inhibitors except fosamprenavir).

From the Colcrys label, Contraindications section.
Open on DailyMed

Why it happens

Colchicine is cleared by CYP3A4 and pumped out by P-glycoprotein (P-gp). Blocking these raises colchicine to toxic levels (diarrhea, muscle damage, bone-marrow failure); fatal cases are reported. The colchicine label contraindicates these inhibitors in patients with kidney or liver impairment and requires a lower dose otherwise.

What to do

Reduce the colchicine dose as the label directs (for gout prophylaxis, 0.3 mg a day or less) or avoid; do not combine at all with kidney or liver impairment.

Ery-Tab, Cardizem CD

Ery-Tab with Cardizem CD: macrolide levels rise: about 5 times the rate of sudden cardiac death

Why D: The Warnings section of the Ery-Tab label addresses this combination: macrolide levels rise: about 5 times the rate of sudden cardiac death. Clinical rulebook row 23.

These include colchicine toxicity with colchicine; rhabdomyolysis with simvastatin, lovastatin, and atorvastatin; and hypotension with calcium channel blockers metabolized by CYP3A4 (e.g., verapamil, amlodipine, diltiazem) (see PRECAUTIONS – Drug Interactions ).

From the Ery-Tab label, Warnings section.
Open on DailyMed

Why it happens

Erythromycin prolongs QT and is cleared by CYP3A4. With a CYP3A4 inhibitor its level rises; users of both had about 5 times the rate of sudden cardiac death (Ray et al., NEJM 2004).

What to do

Avoid; choose azithromycin or a non-macrolide antibiotic.

Why C: The Precautions section of the Lasix label addresses this combination: low potassium and magnesium from the diuretic raise the rhythm risk. Clinical rulebook row 18, 23.

Hypokalemia may develop with furosemide, especially with brisk diuresis, inadequate oral electrolyte intake, when cirrhosis is present, or during concomitant use of corticosteroids, ACTH, licorice in large amounts, or prolonged use of laxatives.

From the Lasix label, Precautions section.

Why it happens

Loop and thiazide diuretics lower potassium and magnesium. Low levels make QT-prolonging drugs more likely to cause torsades and make digoxin more toxic.

What to do

Check potassium and magnesium at the start and after dose changes; replace them to keep potassium above 4.0 mmol/L.
