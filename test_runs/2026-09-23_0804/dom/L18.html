    Elavil, Ditropan, Vistaril, Cogentin, Paxil

    5 anticholinergic drugs: anticholinergic burden: confusion, urinary retention, constipation, falls

    Why D: The Precautions section of the Elavil label addresses this combination: anticholinergic burden: confusion, urinary retention, constipation, falls. Clinical rulebook row 6.

    When benztropine mesylate is given concomitantly with phenothiazines, haloperidol, or other drugs with anticholinergic or antidopaminergic activity, patients should be advised to report gastrointestinal complaints, fever or heat intolerance promptly.

    From the Cogentin label, Warnings section.Open on DailyMed

    Why it happens

    Each drug blocks acetylcholine. Together they cause confusion or delirium, dry mouth, blurred vision, urinary retention, constipation or ileus, overheating, and falls, worst in older adults (Beers criteria).

    What to do

    Replace the strongest anticholinergic with an alternative and avoid the combination in older adults.

    FDA labels: Elavil · Ditropan · Vistaril · Cogentin · Paxil

  2. DConsider changing therapy — Dangerous: avoid, or use only with a specific reason and close monitoring.

    Elavil, Ditropan, Vistaril, Cogentin, Aricept, Paxil

    6 drugs: the anticholinergics work against the dementia drug

    Why D: The Drug Interactions section of the Aricept label addresses this combination: the anticholinergics work against the dementia drug. Clinical rulebook row 6.

    7 DRUG INTERACTIONS Cholinesterase inhibitors have the potential to interfere with the activity of anticholinergic medications (7.1) A synergistic effect may be expected with concomitant administration of succinylcholine, similar neuromuscular blocking agents, or cholinergic agonists (7.2) 7.1 Use with Anticholinergics Because of their mechanism of action, cholinesterase inhibitors have the potential to interfere with the activity of anticholinergic medications.

    From the Aricept label, Drug Interactions section.Open on DailyMed

    Why it happens

    Cholinesterase inhibitors raise acetylcholine to help memory; anticholinergic drugs block acetylcholine. The two work in opposite directions (therapeutic antagonism), so the dementia drug does less and confusion gets worse.

    What to do

    Stop or replace the anticholinergic drugs, or reconsider the cholinesterase inhibitor.

    FDA labels: Elavil · Ditropan · Vistaril · Cogentin · Aricept · Paxil

  3. DConsider changing therapy — Dangerous: avoid, or use only with a specific reason and close monitoring.

    Elavil, Paxil

    Elavil with Paxil: tricyclic levels rise: toxicity, QT prolongation, seizures

    Why D: The Precautions section of the Elavil label addresses this combination: tricyclic levels rise: toxicity, QT prolongation, seizures.

    The drugs that inhibit cytochrome P450 2D6 include some that are not metabolized by the enzyme (quinidine; cimetidine) and many that are substrates for P450 2D6 (many other antidepressants, phenothiazines, and the Type 1C antiarrhythmics propafenone and flecainide).

    From the Elavil label, Precautions section.Open on DailyMed

    Why it happens

    Tricyclics are cleared by CYP2D6. Strong inhibitors can raise tricyclic levels several-fold, causing anticholinergic toxicity, QT prolongation, and seizures.

    What to do

    Use a low tricyclic dose and check a level, or choose another drug.

    FDA labels: Elavil · Paxil · FDA: Drug Development and Drug Interactions, Table of Substrates, Inhibitors and Inducers

    Elavil, Vistaril

    2 CNS depressants: risk of oversedation, slowed breathing (respiratory depression), falls, and overdose

    Why C: Class rule: risk of oversedation, slowed breathing (respiratory depression), falls, and overdose. Clinical rulebook row 1, 2.

    Amitriptyline hydrochloride may enhance the response to alcohol and the effects of barbiturates and other CNS depressants.

    From the Elavil label, Warnings section.Open on DailyMed

    Why it happens

    Each drug depresses the brain on its own; together the effects add up. Opioids with benzodiazepines, gabapentinoids, or other sedatives can stop breathing: both opioid and benzodiazepine labels carry a boxed warning on this combination.

    What to do

    Use the lowest doses for the shortest time, avoid adding more sedatives, and watch for sleepiness and slow or shallow breathing. With an opioid, co-prescribe naloxone.

    FDA labels: Elavil · Vistaril · Phansalkar et al., JAMIA 2012: high-priority drug-drug interactions (ONC expert panel)

  5. CMonitor closely — Clinically significant: adjust the dose or timing, or monitor a named measure.

    Elavil, Paxil

    2 serotonergic drugs: risk of serotonin syndrome

    Why C: The Warnings and Precautions section of the Paxil label addresses this combination: risk of serotonin syndrome. Clinical rulebook row 7.

    5 WARNINGS AND PRECAUTIONS • Serotonin Syndrome: Increased risk when co-administered with other serotonergic agents, but also when taken alone.

    From the Paxil label, Warnings and Precautions section.Open on DailyMed

    Why it happens

    Each drug raises serotonin activity. Together they can cause serotonin syndrome: agitation, tremor, clonus and overactive reflexes, sweating, fever, fast heart rate.

    What to do

    Avoid stacking serotonergic drugs where possible; if needed, use low doses and watch for clonus, tremor, fever, and agitation, especially in the first days after a start or dose increase.

    FDA labels: Elavil · Paxil · Dunkley et al., QJM 2003: the Hunter serotonin toxicity criteria

  6. CMonitor closely — Clinically significant: adjust the dose or timing, or monitor a named measure.

    Elavil, Vistaril

    2 QT-prolonging drugs: risk of QT prolongation and torsades de pointes

    Why C: The Precautions section of the Vistaril label addresses this combination: risk of QT prolongation and torsades de pointes. Clinical rulebook row 23.

    The majority of reports occurred in patients with other risk factors for QT prolongation/TdP (pre-existing heart disease, electrolyte imbalances or concomitant arrhythmogenic drug use).

    From the Vistaril label, Precautions section.Open on DailyMed

    Why it happens

    Each drug slows the heart's electrical recovery (the QT interval). Together the effect adds up and can trigger torsades de pointes, a dangerous heart rhythm.

    What to do

    Get a baseline ECG and keep potassium and magnesium normal. Stop or switch a drug if the QTc passes 500 ms.

    FDA labels: Elavil · Vistaril · Dechanont et al., Pharmacoepidemiol Drug Saf 2014: drug interactions in hospital admissions
