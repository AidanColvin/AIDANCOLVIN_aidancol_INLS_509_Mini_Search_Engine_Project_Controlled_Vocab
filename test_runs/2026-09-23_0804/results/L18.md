Why it happens

Cholinesterase inhibitors raise acetylcholine to help memory; anticholinergic drugs block acetylcholine. The two work in opposite directions (therapeutic antagonism), so the dementia drug does less and confusion gets worse.

Stop or replace the anticholinergic drugs, or reconsider the cholinesterase inhibitor.

Tricyclics are cleared by CYP2D6. Strong inhibitors can raise tricyclic levels several-fold, causing anticholinergic toxicity, QT prolongation, and seizures.

Why C: Class rule: risk of oversedation, slowed breathing (respiratory depression), falls, and overdose. Clinical rulebook row 1, 2.

Amitriptyline hydrochloride may enhance the response to alcohol and the effects of barbiturates and other CNS depressants.

From the Elavil label, Warnings section.

Why it happens

Each drug depresses the brain on its own; together the effects add up. Opioids with benzodiazepines, gabapentinoids, or other sedatives can stop breathing: both opioid and benzodiazepine labels carry a boxed warning on this combination.

What to do

Use the lowest doses for the shortest time, avoid adding more sedatives, and watch for sleepiness and slow or shallow breathing. With an opioid, co-prescribe naloxone.

Why it happens

Each drug raises serotonin activity. Together they can cause serotonin syndrome: agitation, tremor, clonus and overactive reflexes, sweating, fever, fast heart rate.

What to do

Avoid stacking serotonergic drugs where possible; if needed, use low doses and watch for clonus, tremor, fever, and agitation, especially in the first days after a start or dose increase.

The majority of reports occurred in patients with other risk factors for QT prolongation/TdP (pre-existing heart disease, electrolyte imbalances or concomitant arrhythmogenic drug use).

Why it happens

Each drug slows the heart's electrical recovery (the QT interval). Together the effect adds up and can trigger torsades de pointes, a dangerous heart rhythm.

What to do

Get a baseline ECG and keep potassium and magnesium normal. Stop or switch a drug if the QTc passes 500 ms.
